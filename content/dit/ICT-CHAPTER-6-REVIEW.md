## Syllabus and teaching material

The official PBTE two-year DIT first-semester outline, page 8, lists five sections: 6.1 application-software need and role; 6.2 working with applications; 6.3 business, graphics and multimedia software; 6.4 web applications; 6.5 typing assistance software.

The business/multimedia section explicitly covers word processing, spreadsheets, databases, presentations, project management, personal information managers, smartphone business applications, accounting, CAD, DTP, paint/image editing, video editing, audio editing, multimedia authoring and web page authoring. Each category is represented in its 15-question bank. Web applications also have 15 questions; sections 6.1, 6.2 and 6.5 have ten each. Total: 60 original explained MCQs, five detailed English topic guides, 12 short answers, six long answers, five practical activities, an application workflow diagram and three comparison tables.

The separate learning test samples four MCQs from each syllabus section, giving 20 MCQs, ten short questions and three long questions. The answer key is separate and repeated revision questions are disclosed. No official paper pattern, past-paper appearance or examination guarantee is invented.

## Accuracy and source review

The guide distinguishes application programs from their data and delivery method from task category. It separates editing, formatting, saving, exporting, printing and uninstalling. Editable project structure and required source assets are distinguished from flattened delivery files. Business categories can overlap; spreadsheet formulas, queries, accounting entries and design dimensions still require review.

Web applications are not assumed to be server-only, universally offline-capable or automatically private. Save/sync state, sharing roles, conflict handling, version history, independent backup, export compatibility, browser/device support and sign-out are treated separately. HTTPS is not presented as proof of every site's promises. No fixed service price, feature set or quota is promised.

Typing examples explicitly state a five-character gross-WPM convention and a separate final-text accuracy method. The worked examples, 250 characters in one minute yielding 50 gross WPM and 245 matched out of 250 yielding 98 percent, are calculated in checks. Error-adjusted/net scoring is not falsely described as universal. Layout and test conditions are recorded for comparison; no score guarantees employment or examination marks.

References include PBTE, LibreOffice, Microsoft Project, Audacity, MDN, TypingClub, government vocational typing material, GIMP and Autodesk. Practical tasks use approved existing tools and non-sensitive examples, with paper alternatives where an application or authorised account is unavailable.

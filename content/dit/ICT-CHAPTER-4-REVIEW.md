## Accuracy and practical review

The guide separates capacity, latency and throughput; decimal GB and binary GiB; physical drives and logical volumes; RAID, NAS, sync and backup; form factor, interface and protocol; optical read/write compatibility; tape positioning and sustained streaming. It explains that ExpressCard is an expansion format whose modules have different functions, rather than calling every module flash storage. Optical capacities are labelled common examples with layer/format caveats. Compressed tape capacity is conditional on the data. Cloud storage is physical remote infrastructure with account, permission and retention responsibilities; no fixed pricing or quota is promised.

References include PBTE, Seagate, IBM, Dell, Kingston, Sonnet, Panasonic, Microsoft, NIST and a Viking Technology flash endurance application note. RAID 0, RAID 1 and RAID 5 are distinguished. The equal-drive RAID 1 capacity example and GiB byte multiplier have explicit checks. Recovery exercises retain the source and verify a separate sample copy. They do not ask students to format, repartition or open storage devices, or buy subscriptions.

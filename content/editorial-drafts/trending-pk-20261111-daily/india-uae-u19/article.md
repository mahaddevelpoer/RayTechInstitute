## Run rate and required rate are different measures

Current run rate describes scoring already achieved per over. Required run rate describes the remaining scoring task per over available. During a chase, both can be useful, but they answer different questions. A single displayed number should not be quoted without its label.

Use legal balls for the calculation. An over notation such as 18.3 represents eighteen completed overs and three legal balls, which is 111 legal balls. It is not the ordinary decimal number 18.3. Dividing runs by a decimal interpretation gives the wrong cricket rate.

As a hypothetical example, needing 60 runs from 60 legal balls means requiring six per over. If a wicket falls, the arithmetic requirement may remain the same while the available batting resources change. This shows why a rate is informative without being a complete prediction of the result.

## New Zealand's next India ODIs are at Bay Oval

New Zealand and India are scheduled to play the fourth and fifth ODIs at Bay Oval, Mount Maunganui, on Friday 13 November and Sunday 15 November 2026. Both the venue and the published international schedule confirm those dates. Their displayed start times differ, however, so the timing needs a fresh match-centre check before setting an alarm.

This preview focuses on the final two one-day matches, New Zealand's announced split-squad plan and what to watch in a fifty-over contest. It is not a report of a match that has already happened. Earlier Auckland and Hamilton articles cover different fixtures and should not be mistaken for this Bay Oval weekend.

For Pakistan-based readers, the practical questions are straightforward: which date, which ground, which squad and what time? The dates and ground are established. The two conflicting time listings are set out below without disguising the difference or treating one as a confirmed revision of the other.

The venue explicitly calls its time “first ball”; an editor should not relabel it merely to make the figures agree.

Check the live match centre closest to the game and distinguish scheduled start from toss, gates opening and actual start. Weather or other conditions can change the last of those without changing the original fixture date.

## Pakistan time: both conversions, clearly labelled

The November daylight-saving offset in mainland New Zealand is UTC+13; Pakistan uses UTC+5 and India UTC+5:30. Mount Maunganui is therefore eight hours ahead of Pakistan and seven and a half hours ahead of India. The New Zealand government lists the current daylight-saving period as 27 September 2026 to 4 April 2027.

## Why New Zealand announced two ODI squads

The ICC's 11 October announcement describes a split between ODIs one to three and ODIs four to five. Mitchell Santner was named captain of both groups. The plan allowed some red-ball players to leave for Wellington after the third ODI and prepare for the first Test, scheduled to begin on 19 November.

The announced final-two-match group included Finn Allen, Lockie Ferguson and Tim Seifert, alongside players continuing from the earlier games. The October list is a selection announcement, not a guaranteed playing eleven or a certificate that every selected player remains available on match day. Subsequent injury and replacement notices take priority.

A split squad can serve several purposes at once: protect preparation time, provide opportunities to other players and maintain options across formats. That does not automatically mean a match is less important. A replacement may be competing for a longer-term role, while a captain still has to field a balanced side.

For readers comparing team lists, attach the match range to the list. “New Zealand ODI squad” is too broad if one article refers to games one to three and another to games four and five. A missing name may reflect the announced changeover rather than a new injury or a disciplinary decision.

## Squad selection is not the same as the playing eleven

The squad is the pool from which the match team is selected. The playing eleven identifies those chosen for a particular game. A player can be included in a squad without appearing, and a player omitted from an earlier match can still be selected later.

Wait for the official toss-time team sheet before describing someone as opening the batting or sharing the new ball. A predicted eleven is an opinion, even when it comes from a knowledgeable analyst. It should not be placed beside confirmed information without a clear label.

This distinction matters for scorecards too. A squad profile may show a player's career record while a match page shows no contribution because he did not play. Absence from the batting list does not, by itself, establish whether he was outside the eleven, did not need to bat or was affected by another match circumstance.

## What changes between a T20I, an ODI and a Test?

The same national sides can meet in three different formats during one tour. A T20I is built around a shorter innings; an ODI provides a longer limited-overs contest; a Test can develop across multiple days and innings. Their records should remain separate.

A strong innings in the preceding T20I series is relevant background, but it is not an ODI score. Similarly, a Test preparation decision does not establish that an ODI player has retired from another format. Check the competition label before comparing results, averages or team roles.

In an ODI, batters usually have more time than in a T20I to rebuild after early wickets. Bowlers also work with a different allocation and different phases of the innings. The general problem remains the balance between scoring quickly and preserving enough resources to finish strongly.

## Three questions to watch when New Zealand bat

First, can the opening pair establish scoring options without handing away wickets? This is not simply a choice between attacking and defending every ball. A well-judged leave, a single into space and a boundary from a poor delivery can all contribute to the same plan.

Second, how does the middle order respond if the early plan breaks down? A partnership after wickets may need to reduce risk temporarily. That phase becomes more useful if it creates an opportunity to accelerate later, rather than leaving every remaining batter to start under immediate pressure.

Third, who is available for the closing overs? A side with established batters at the crease can approach the finish differently from a side relying on its last pair. The total on the board is only one part of the position; wickets and deliveries remaining help explain what might still be possible.

These are watching questions, not assertions about an unplayed innings. There is no confirmed pitch outcome or guaranteed scoring pattern in this preview. Assess the actual conditions and decisions when the match begins.

## Three questions to watch when New Zealand bowl

Begin with control: are the bowlers repeatedly reaching a useful line and length, or offering easy scoring opportunities? Pace alone cannot answer that. A fast delivery in the wrong place may be less effective than a controlled ball that makes the batter choose carefully.

Next, consider the partnership between bowlers. Pressure from one end can change the risks a batter takes at the other. A wicket belongs in an individual's figures, but the preceding spell may have helped create the situation. The scorecard and the sequence together give a fuller account.

Finally, watch how the field changes after a boundary or wicket. A captain may protect one area, bring a catcher closer or keep the existing arrangement. Those decisions reveal the problem being addressed, although they do not prove what the bowler and captain said privately.

Do not infer a player’s fitness from speed readings or a single short spell. That requires a reliable team update. Tactical observations should describe the visible cricket rather than becoming unsupported medical explanations.

## Reading an ODI chase without confusing the arithmetic

Suppose a hypothetical team needs 90 from 60 legal deliveries. Its required rate is nine an over. If it then scores ten from an over, it needs 80 from 54 balls, or about 8.89 an over. The situation has changed even though the remaining target still looks substantial.

Wickets alter the interpretation. Two batters well set with eight wickets available face a different problem from a last pair needing the same number. The arithmetic provides the rate; the match situation provides the risk attached to reaching it.

Overs are not decimal fractions. An entry of 32.4 means thirty-two completed overs plus four legal balls. Treating it as 32.4 ordinary units produces an incorrect rate. Convert to balls first if doing a detailed calculation, then convert back to overs when needed.

Any worked numbers in this section are examples, not a forecast or a score from the Bay Oval matches. If rain changes an innings, use the officially revised target and remaining allocation rather than continuing with the original chase calculation.

## Scores, highlights and streaming are different services

A live scorecard can provide a legal-ball count, wickets, individual figures and match status without offering video. Highlights provide selected moments rather than the complete game. A broadcaster's live coverage is a third service with its own territorial rights and access conditions.

New Zealand Cricket links to its official scoring service. Use the match date and format to select the right fixture. A page for an earlier game or an A-team contest can otherwise look similar while describing a different event entirely.

This preview does not claim a verified free Pakistan video stream. Before subscribing to a service, check that the rights apply in your country and include this particular series. A general cricket channel listing or a free trial elsewhere is not enough to establish that access.

Avoid websites that demand unrelated downloads to show a score or promise unrestricted access without identifying an authorised broadcaster. The useful information is the verified fixture and match record, not a link that misrepresents what it supplies.

No. The published dates are Friday 13 November and Sunday 15 November, leaving Saturday between them. A two-match weekend description does not mean that both games take place on the same day.

The two official listings currently convert differently. The venue's time gives 6:30am Pakistan time; BCCI's published time gives 7:00am. Check the latest match centre before relying on either. The conversions are clear, but the underlying discrepancy remains unresolved.

No. It identifies the selected group for the specified match range. Later updates and the match-day eleven must be checked separately. Do not treat an announcement published weeks earlier as proof that no subsequent replacement has occurred.

## Why the result is a four-wicket win

Sri Lanka finished six wickets down. From the normal ten available wickets, four remained, so the result is a win by four wickets. The margin describes remaining batting resources, not a subtraction between the two teams' wicket columns.

This is different from a win by runs. A defending side wins by runs when the opposition finishes short of its target under the applicable match conditions. Here, Sri Lanka completed the chase, so the result is expressed in wickets.

## What does 18.1 overs mean?

It means 18 complete overs and one legal delivery, which equals 109 legal balls. It is not a decimal measurement of eighteen and one tenth overs. Cricket writes the number of balls after the point, and a six-ball over ends when that count reaches six.

A full twenty-over allowance contains 120 legal deliveries. Subtracting 109 leaves 11, matching the completed report's balls-remaining figure. The calculation is a useful consistency check because it connects the final overs notation with the stated margin in deliveries.

Wides and no-balls complicate the raw number of deliveries physically bowled because they do not normally consume a legal ball. For basic scorecard arithmetic, use legal deliveries rather than counting every line in a commentary feed as if it reduced the allowance equally.

## How bowling figures differ from batting scores

In a bowling table, three for 18 means three credited wickets and 18 conceded runs. In a batting table, a slash notation usually concerns the team score and wickets lost. The surrounding heading tells you which meaning applies.

Economy rate measures runs conceded per over, while bowling strike rate measures legal balls per wicket. Bowling average measures runs per wicket. These are different questions and should not be merged into a single unlabeled number.

For a fictional spell of two wickets for 24 from four complete overs, economy is six runs per over, average is 12 runs per wicket and strike rate is 12 balls per wicket. Writing the units beside each answer makes the distinctions easier to remember.

## Why a near-century partnership is not required for a valuable stand

A useful partnership can be short in runs but decisive in its circumstances. A small unbeaten stand can complete a chase after earlier wickets. A rebuilding stand can keep an innings alive even if neither batter reaches a personal landmark.

The partnership total includes the team's runs while the two batters are together, potentially including extras. It is not necessarily identical to the sum of their final individual scores because each may have batted with other partners.

When explaining a finish, identify the stand from the actual partnership record rather than adding whichever two individual totals look relevant. If the available report does not give a stand's value, describe the batters' contributions without inventing an exact partnership figure.

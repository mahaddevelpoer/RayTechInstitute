## Reading three for eight correctly

The phrase means three wickets credited to the bowler for eight runs conceded in the stated spell. It does not give the overs on its own. Without the legal-ball count, the reader should not invent an economy rate for that return.

This is an important limit. Runs divided by wickets can give a spell average where wickets were taken, but economy needs overs or legal balls. A statistic requiring a missing input cannot be reconstructed simply because the other numbers look small.

The same discipline applies to a social graphic. If the original return lacks one field, leave that field out or consult the full scorecard. A complete-looking but guessed line is less useful than a shorter accurate record.

## Wickets are not the only part of an over

A bowler may concede few runs without taking a wicket in a particular over. Another over may produce a wicket and more runs. The role of each depends on the match task, so a useful review looks beyond a single result label.

## Economy and wicket-taking are separate measures

Economy measures runs per over, while bowling average measures runs per wicket. Bowling strike rate measures legal balls per wicket. They can describe different aspects of a spell and should not be used as interchangeable names for one number.

For a fictional four-over spell costing 20 runs with two wickets, economy is five, average is ten and strike rate is twelve balls per wicket. Write the units beside each answer. The labels explain why the three values are not competing versions of the same calculation.

## The field supports a bowling plan

A delivery is played within a field. The same ball can present different scoring options depending on where fielders stand. For an observation exercise, draw the visible field before explaining a shot rather than discussing the ball in isolation.

Then ask which areas look open and which are covered. Keep the answer descriptive. A visible gap does not prove what the batter or bowler intended, and a fielder's position does not guarantee the ball will travel there.

This is particularly useful when studying spin because the relationship between length, shot choice and field can change across an over. The explanation becomes clearer when it names the visible elements instead of relying on a broad phrase such as clever bowling.

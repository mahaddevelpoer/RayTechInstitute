## A simple score-reading exercise for match day

Use a fictional 50-over example: a team makes 240 and the chase is unchanged. The target to win is 241. If the chasing side finishes on 241 for six, the margin is four wickets. If it is all out for 220, the defending side wins by 20 runs.

These invented figures are not a forecast for the final. They demonstrate why the innings order and target matter when interpreting the result. Subtracting two totals is appropriate for a completed defending victory in this example, while a successful chase uses wickets remaining.

## Keep over notation separate from decimals

If an innings shows 21.5 overs, that means 21 completed overs and five legal balls, or 131 legal balls. It does not mean 21.5 decimal overs. This is important when calculating run rate or comparing how long a chase lasted.

## Why are substitutions part of the story?

The Juventus report lists Zhegrova replacing Kolo Muani and Boga replacing Alajbegovic. It also describes contributions from those players to the goals. That establishes participation and specific reported actions. It does not prove that every substitution automatically improves a team or that the same change would work in every match.

In football, a substitution can alter the skills, energy or roles available to a side. To explain its effect in a particular game, identify an observable action rather than relying only on the fact that a goal occurred later. “A substitute scored” is a narrower claim than “the entire tactical plan was transformed.”

For a fictional team discussion, give three substitute profiles: a player comfortable receiving between lines, one who offers width and one who helps defend space. Ask which might address a stated problem. There need not be one universal answer. The quality of the explanation depends on connecting the proposed role to the problem.

## What is the difference between a contribution and a cause?

A player’s final pass or finish can be described as a contribution to a scoring move. A broader statement about why the entire match was won requires more evidence. Football involves several interacting decisions, and a single event should not automatically become the explanation for everything that followed.

Imagine a fictional match where a substitute scores with their first touch. The event shows an immediate scoring contribution. It does not, on its own, establish whether the team had already been creating chances, whether the opponent made a mistake or whether a change in shape caused the opening.

Ask students to write two versions of a summary: one describing the event and one offering an interpretation. Underline the extra evidence the interpretation needs. This is a useful reasoning exercise because it allows analysis while showing where a writer moves beyond a straightforward record of what happened.

A description of shot location can help explain the visible action, but location alone does not supply a probability of scoring. An expected-goals estimate would require a defined model and the relevant inputs, not just the phrase “long-range effort.”

For an original observation task, draw a pitch and mark several fictional shooting locations. Clear limits make the diagram more informative, because readers know what it is designed to explain and what it cannot establish.

## What does a second yellow card change?

A dismissal changes the number of players available to a side under the applicable match rules. In this fixture, the reports identify Marianucci’s second booking as leading to his dismissal. The event belongs in the account, but it should not be described as the cause of an earlier goal that had already been scored.

The general football question is how a side reorganises when it has fewer players. It may need to cover similar spaces with different responsibilities. The specific response requires observation or reporting. This article does not invent Napoli’s exact positional adjustments from the red-card entry alone.

Use a fictional board with eleven markers, then remove one. Ask learners to identify which spaces might become harder to cover and what information they would need before choosing a new shape. The exercise is about a constrained decision, not a claim that every ten-player team should use the same arrangement.

## A scoreless first half is still information

The club report describes chances before the interval, including an effort against the post, while recording no first-half goal. This distinction is important: attempts and goals are different events. A period can contain attacking actions without changing the score, and the lack of a goal does not establish that neither team tried to attack.

For a fictional exercise, give two halves the same 0-0 score but different lists of attempts. Ask learners which conclusions the score supports and which require the event list. The exercise illustrates how a summary can be accurate yet incomplete without being wrong.

When writing your own account, avoid adding a detailed shot count from memory or from a short compilation. If a complete and reliable dataset is unavailable, describe the verified events you have. An article does not become more accurate merely because its layout contains more statistical boxes.

## Why can club and league reports sound different?

A club report is written from a participant’s perspective, while a league report may organise its account differently. They can agree on core events and still emphasise different moments. A writer should compare the actual claims rather than assuming that a difference in tone is automatically a factual contradiction.

Where the records genuinely differ, identify the field involved. A minor displayed minute difference is not the same kind of issue as a different scorer or final score. Preserve the common verified facts and explain material uncertainty. Do not quietly combine incompatible details into a smoother but unsupported story.

For a media-literacy worksheet, provide two fictional reports of the same match. Ask students to list shared facts, unique details and genuine conflicts. This helps them use several sources thoughtfully rather than choosing the one with the most confident headline or the most enthusiastic language.

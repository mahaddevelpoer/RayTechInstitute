Norway beat Wales 3-1 at Ullevaal Stadion in Oslo on 14 November 2026 to leave Craig Bellamy's side bottom of UEFA Nations League Group A4. Antonio Nusa opened the scoring in the 59th minute, Andreas Schjelderup doubled the lead in the 68th, David Brooks pulled one back in the 79th and Erling Haaland sealed the win with a penalty in the 88th.

## Norway 3-1 Wales: the result in context

This was a League A Group 4 match with consequences at both ends of the table. Norway needed a win to keep alive their slim chance of catching Denmark in second place. Wales needed something from the game to keep their own hopes of a top-three finish alive, and to protect their seeding for the European Championship draw.

Instead, Wales left Oslo with nothing. They are now guaranteed to finish fourth in the group regardless of what happens in their final match against Portugal in Cardiff on Tuesday. Sky Sports reported that Wales cannot climb off the bottom because their head-to-head record is worse than both Denmark's and Norway's, the two teams they could have drawn level with on six points.

The match itself was more competitive than the final score suggests. Wales were the better side for long stretches of the first half and created the clearest chances before the break, mostly through Daniel James. They did not take them. Norway, who had been passive and untidy in possession, improved after half-time and scored three times in the final half hour.

That pattern is familiar in international football. A team that plays well without scoring can lose confidence quickly when the opponent scores first. Wales's structure held for an hour, then came apart in a ten-minute spell either side of the second goal. By the time Brooks scored his superb chip, Norway had already done enough to control the game.

UEFA records the fixture as a League A Group 4 match at Ullevaal Stadion, Oslo, on Saturday 14 November 2026, with a 17:00 local kick-off. The half-time score was 0-0. All four goals arrived in the second half.

## How the three Norway goals arrived

The first goal came from the kind of individual moment Wales had been able to contain until then. Antonio Nusa received the ball on the left, moved inside and struck a low shot into the bottom-left corner. Sky Sports described it as a fine strike and noted that Nusa is often called the Norwegian Neymar. The finish was clean, but the space he was allowed to move into was the bigger issue for Wales.

The second goal was simpler and more damaging. Haaland received the ball and played a pass across the box for Schjelderup, a substitute, to tap in from close range. Wales's back line had been pulled toward the ball, and nobody tracked the runner arriving at the far post. Two goals in nine minutes turned a tight game into a difficult one.

Wales responded well. Brooks, another substitute, scored with a chipped finish that gave Norway's goalkeeper no chance. For a few minutes, the game looked as if it might become uncomfortable for the hosts. Wales pushed players forward and Norway dropped deeper.

The penalty ended that momentum. Connor Roberts pushed Schjelderup in the box, and the referee pointed to the spot. Haaland took responsibility and converted in the 88th minute. Sky Sports framed the decision as a clear push, and there was no suggestion of a lengthy VAR review in the report. The goal restored Norway's two-goal cushion and removed any realistic chance of a Welsh equaliser.

## Wales's first half and the chances that went begging

The most important part of this match for Wales was not the last ten minutes. It was the first 45.

Wales looked the better team in that period. They pressed with purpose, moved the ball through midfield and found space behind Norway's full-backs. Daniel James was the focal point of that work. Sky Sports reported that he had three chances to give Wales the lead. Those chances came in different ways, which suggests the plan was working rather than relying on one lucky break.

Bellamy's team also defended well in the first half without sitting especially deep. Norway had territory but not many clear openings. Haaland was isolated for long periods. Nusa and the other Norwegian attackers received the ball in wide areas rather than in the box.

The failure to score changed the psychology of the game. Wales went into half-time level but without the reward their performance deserved. Norway, by contrast, had a platform to improve. International teams with players of Haaland's quality rarely stay quiet for 90 minutes, especially at home.

Bellamy was measured afterwards. He said there was "loads for us to improve on" but that he liked a lot of what he saw. He praised the pressure on the ball in the first half, the way Wales moved the ball when they triggered the press, and the impact of his substitutes. He also admitted that Wales lost momentum and energy in the second half, which he called a difficult period.

That is a fair summary. Wales did not lose because they were passive. They lost because they could not convert their best spell into a goal, and because Norway's best spell produced three.

Portugal have already qualified for the quarter-finals. Denmark and Norway will decide second place in Copenhagen on Tuesday. Wales are bottom and cannot move.

The relegation rules are more complicated than a simple bottom-two go down. Wales are guaranteed to finish last in Group A4, but that does not automatically mean relegation to League B. Under the Nations League format, the two worst bottom-placed teams across League A are relegated automatically. The others go to a relegation play-off against a League B runner-up in March.

Wales have three points. Sky Sports reported that the other bottom-placed sides in League A currently have zero points. That gives Wales a strong chance of avoiding automatic relegation, even though they cannot escape last place in their own group. A draw against Portugal on Tuesday would guarantee a play-off place rather than automatic relegation, according to Sky Sports.

The play-off would be against a League B runner-up. That is a difficult draw but not a fatal one. Wales would have two matches to preserve their League A status, and they would go in with the knowledge that they have already competed with three strong teams in this group.

## What relegation would mean for Wales

The Nations League is not just a separate competition with a trophy at the end. It feeds into seeding and play-off routes for major tournaments. Wales's position in League A has been part of their effort to secure a favourable draw for Euro 2028 qualifying.

Bellamy was explicit about that before the match. He said Wales wanted to be in pot one for the European Championship draw in Belfast next month. He also acknowledged that some people might question whether Wales deserve that status, but he made clear that he believed they did.

Finishing bottom of Group A4 makes that goal much harder. Sky Sports reported that Wales knew finishing in the top three would put them in pot one for the Euros draw. Now they will not finish in the top three. The play-off route may still keep them in League A, but their seeding will be affected.

There is a wider context too. Wales will co-host Euro 2028 alongside England, Scotland and the Republic of Ireland. If they qualify, they are due to open the tournament in Cardiff. That is a huge opportunity for the team and for the country. But co-hosting does not guarantee qualification, and Wales still need to build a team capable of competing at that level.

The result in Oslo does not destroy that project. It does expose the gap between playing well in phases and turning those phases into results. Bellamy's team is young in places, and the coach has been willing to make bold substitutions. The second-half performance against Norway showed both the promise and the risk of that approach.

## Norway's night: Haaland, Nusa and Schjelderup

Norway's win was built on the quality of their front players and on a much better second-half performance.

Nusa's goal was the breakthrough. He has been one of Norway's most exciting attackers for several years, and the comparison with Neymar, however loose, reflects his ability to beat defenders in one-on-one situations. Wales had handled him reasonably well until the 59th minute. Then he found half a yard and finished low into the corner.

Haaland did not have a dominant game by his standards, but he was involved in the two decisive moments after the first goal. He provided the pass for Schjelderup's tap-in and he converted the penalty. That is what top strikers do in tight matches. They may be quiet for an hour, but they affect the score when the game opens up.

Schjelderup's contribution off the bench was significant. He scored the second goal and won the penalty. Substitutes who change the game in that way are invaluable in international football, where squad depth is often the difference between a good campaign and a great one.

Norway still have a chance to finish second. They travel to Denmark on Tuesday knowing that a win would move them level on points with the Danes. The head-to-head record and goal difference would then decide the order. That is a narrow path, but it is a path.

For Norway, the tournament has been a story of inconsistency. They have won two and lost three. They have scored nine and conceded nine. The win over Wales keeps them in the fight, but it does not hide the fact that they have been beaten by Portugal and Denmark in this group.

## What this result does and does not prove

It does not prove that Wales are a bad team. They were the better side in the first half and created the best chances. They have competed in a difficult group and have a clear identity under Bellamy.

It does not prove that Norway are contenders for the Nations League title. They are third in their group and need a result in Copenhagen to finish second. Their performance in the first half was poor, and a better opponent would have punished them.

It does not settle the debate about Wales's seeding or their Euro 2028 prospects. One match in Oslo is part of a longer process. The play-off in March and the qualifying campaign will matter more.

What it does prove is that Wales still lack a cutting edge. Three goals in five group matches is not enough at this level. Daniel James caused problems, Brooks scored a brilliant goal, and the team created chances, but the conversion rate was not good enough. That is the central lesson for Bellamy and his staff.

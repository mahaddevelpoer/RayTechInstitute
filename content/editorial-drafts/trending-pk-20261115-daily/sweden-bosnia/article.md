Sweden beat Bosnia and Herzegovina 3-2 at Strawberry Arena in Solna on 14 November 2026 to win UEFA Nations League Group B4 and secure promotion to League A. Viktor Gyökeres scored two penalties, Daniel Svensson scored the opener and Bosnia fought back from 3-0 down with second-half goals from Armin Gigović and Sead Kolašinac.

## Sweden 3-2 Bosnia: the result in context

This was the match that decided the group. Sweden needed a win to guarantee first place. Bosnia needed a result to keep their own hopes of overtaking Sweden alive. The first half settled the contest in Sweden's favour, and the second half became a test of whether Graham Potter's side could survive a comeback.

They could. Sweden led 3-0 at half-time, conceded twice in the first 17 minutes of the second half and then held on in front of a record crowd at the Strawberry Arena. The Swedish Football Association reported an attendance of 51,049, a new record for the stadium and the third-highest attendance for a Sweden men's home match.

The result means Sweden finish the group on 13 points from five matches and will play in League A of the next Nations League in autumn 2028. Bosnia finish second on eight points and will enter a promotion play-off in March 2027.

The match was played on Saturday 14 November 2026 at Strawberry Arena, Solna, with a 20:45 CET kick-off. The half-time score was 3-0, and the full-time score was 3-2.

## How the first half was won

Sweden started quickly and were ahead inside five minutes. Daniel Svensson scored the opener, with Anthony Elanga providing the assist. It was the kind of early goal that settles a home team and forces the opponent to change its plan.

The second goal came from the penalty spot in the 11th minute. Gyökeres converted to make it 2-0. The Swedish Football Association's report does not describe the incident in detail, but the scoreline and the timing are clear: Sweden had a two-goal lead before Bosnia had settled.

Sweden thought they had scored a third when Alexander Isak finished after good work from Elanga and Gyökeres. The goal was reviewed by VAR and disallowed for offside. That decision kept Bosnia within reach for a few more minutes.

The reprieve did not last. In stoppage time of the first half, Svensson was brought down in the box and Sweden were awarded another penalty. Gyökeres converted again, this time in the second minute of added time, to send Sweden into the break with a 3-0 lead.

Two penalties in one half is unusual, but the underlying pattern was not. Sweden attacked with purpose, got into dangerous areas and forced Bosnia into defensive decisions. The goals came from a flowing move, a penalty and a set-piece situation that produced another penalty. That is a varied attacking performance.

Gyökeres deserves credit for the penalties. Taking two spot kicks in the same half, with the scoreline changing after each one, is a test of concentration as much as technique. He converted both, and the second one arrived in stoppage time when Bosnia were already looking for the half-time whistle. Svensson's contribution was different but equally important. He scored the opener and won the second penalty, which means he was directly involved in two of Sweden's three first-half goals.

## How Bosnia fought back

Bosnia's response after half-time was strong. Armin Gigović scored in the 47th minute, just two minutes after the restart, with Ermin Mahmić providing the assist. Gigović was born in Lund and came through Landskrona BoIS, and he has represented Sweden at under-21 level. His goal gave Bosnia hope and changed the mood in the stadium.

The second goal arrived in the 62nd minute. Captain Sead Kolašinac headed in from a corner, with Kerim Alajbegović providing the assist. Suddenly Sweden's comfortable lead was down to one goal, and Bosnia were winning the physical battles.

The Swedish Football Association's report described Bosnia winning duels on the ground and, more importantly, in the air. Potter acknowledged the shift afterwards. "After their two goals we needed to survive and we did," he said.

Sweden survived because of a few decisive defensive moments. Their goalkeeper made an important save. Gustaf Lagerbielke cleared a ball from his own penalty area with a bicycle kick. Bosnia pushed players forward and created half-chances, but they could not find an equaliser.

The final whistle confirmed a win that was more nervous than it needed to be. Sweden had controlled the first half and survived the second. That is enough to win a group, but it is not a performance that removes all questions.

Sweden have already won the group. Bosnia are guaranteed second place. Poland and Romania are playing for third place and for pride.

The final round is on Tuesday 17 November 2026. Bosnia host Romania in Zenica. Poland host Sweden in Wroclaw. Those matches will not change Sweden's position at the top, but they will affect the play-off picture and the final standings.

The promotion system is straightforward. The group winner moves up to League A. The second and third placed teams enter promotion and relegation play-offs in March 2027. Bosnia, as runners-up, will have a chance to reach League A through those play-offs.

## Sweden's promotion in perspective

Sweden's Nations League campaign has been strong without being spectacular. They have won four and drawn one. They have scored 10 and conceded five. They have not lost a match.

The group was not the strongest in League B, but Sweden did what good teams do: they took points from the teams around them and avoided defeat. They drew 1-1 in Zenica against Bosnia on 2 October and won 1-0 in Bucharest against Romania on 5 October. Those results gave them a platform before the home matches, and the win over Bosnia completed the job. The 3-2 win over Bosnia was their fifth match and their fourth win. It was also their narrowest win of the campaign so far.

Promotion to League A means Sweden will face stronger opposition in the next edition, in autumn 2028. That is both an opportunity and a risk. Stronger opponents will punish the kind of second-half lapse that allowed Bosnia back into this match. But a higher level of competition is also the best way to prepare for major tournaments.

For Potter, the campaign provides a platform. He has a settled attacking structure built around Gyökeres, Isak and Elanga, and a midfield that can control games. The defensive phase is less settled. The second-half performance against Bosnia exposed gaps between the lines and a susceptibility to set pieces.

Potter's own assessment was honest. He did not pretend the performance was perfect. He said Sweden needed to survive after Bosnia's two goals, and he was right. Surviving is what matters in the table, but it is not the same as controlling a game.

## The Bosnia view: encouragement in defeat

Bosnia leave the group with eight points and a place in the promotion play-offs. That is a respectable outcome for a team that was drawn against a strong Sweden side.

Their first half in Solna was poor. They conceded early, gave away two penalties and went into the break three goals down. At that point, the match looked over.

Their group form had been solid before this match. They drew 1-1 with Sweden in Zenica on 2 October and beat Poland 1-0 in Zenica on 5 October. Those results were why second place was still within reach going into the final away match.

Their second half was much better. Gigović's goal gave them belief. Kolašinac's header made the score 3-2 and forced Sweden to defend. Bosnia won duels and set pieces, and they came close to an equaliser.

The lesson for Bosnia is that they can compete with Sweden when they play with intensity. The problem is that they gave themselves too much to do. In a group where the margins are small, a slow start can be fatal.

The play-offs in March 2027 give them another route to League A. They will need to reproduce the second-half performance, not the first-half one, if they are to take it.

## What this result does and does not prove

It does not prove that Sweden are ready to compete at the top of League A. They beat a good Bosnia team, but they nearly threw away a three-goal lead. Stronger opponents will create more chances from the spaces Sweden left after half-time.

It does not prove that Bosnia are a bad team. They showed character and quality in the second half. Their problem was the first 45 minutes, not the overall level of the squad.

It does not settle the debate about Potter's tactics. Sweden's attacking plan worked well in the first half. The defensive plan was exposed in the second. Both things can be true.

What it does prove is that Sweden can win a group. They were the best team in Group B4 over five matches. They took 13 points from 15 and did not lose a match. That is the definition of a successful campaign.

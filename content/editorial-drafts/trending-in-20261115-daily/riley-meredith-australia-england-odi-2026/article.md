### Why Australia rested Mahli Beardman after six wickets

The apparent contradiction in search results is simple: Beardman did exceptionally well in Perth, yet Australia still left him out in Adelaide. Cricket Australia reported that the young quick was being managed as he built his capacity following injuries. Its live coverage said Beardman had previously spent seven months out with a recurrence of a stress fracture. The ICC's announcement similarly described workload management rather than a loss of form. A selection decision can protect a bowler's longer-term availability even when his most recent match was outstanding.

Beardman's Perth figures were 6/24 from 7.4 overs. England were dismissed for 131 and Australia won by seven wickets. Those figures explain why his Adelaide absence generated questions: a casual reader might assume a six-wicket player would be an automatic repeat selection. The selectors were balancing that recent performance against physical preparation and the short gap between ODIs. Adelaide followed only two days after Perth. We should not turn the official explanation into a new diagnosis; the reliable claim is the announced workload plan.

The ICC said Beardman was expected back for the third ODI in Hobart on the following Wednesday. “Expected” is a forward-looking statement, not proof that he ultimately played. This article preserves that distinction because a squad preview can become inaccurate if written as a settled result. His planned return also shows why Meredith's arrival did not necessarily mean Beardman had lost his place for the rest of the series. One match's selection and a whole series' roster are different questions.

### How Meredith's squad call-up became an Adelaide start

Before a match, an expanded squad gives selectors options. Meredith was called into that group as a pace option. Cricket Australia's coverage named him and Liam Scott as possible replacements for Beardman before the toss. When Mitchell Marsh announced the side, the confirmed change was Meredith for Beardman. It listed Meredith alongside Marsh, Travis Head, Cooper Connolly, Josh Inglis, Matt Renshaw, Cameron Green, Jack Edwards, Xavier Bartlett, Adam Zampa and Spencer Johnson.

Second, an alternative named in a preview should not later be described as if he was selected. Scott was an option in the advance discussion, but Meredith's name appears in the toss-confirmed XI. For readers searching “Did Riley Meredith play against England in Adelaide?”, the answer is yes: he was in Australia's starting eleven for the second ODI.

Marsh elected to bowl first after winning the toss. ### Who is Riley Meredith?

### Meredith versus Beardman: what the change does and does not mean

Both players are pace bowlers, which is why the substitution preserved a broad bowling role. Beardman had just recorded the headline figures in Perth. Meredith offered experience from previous international appearances and years in the domestic game. However, it would be misleading to present the choice as a referendum on which bowler is better. The official explanation centres on Beardman's workload, not an adverse comparison of ability. ### Nathan Ellis and Ben Dwarshuis: a separate squad change

Some coverage placed three names in the same headline: Meredith, Ellis and Dwarshuis. They belong to different parts of the tour. The [ICC update](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/australia-make-squad-changes-for-england-odis-and-t20is) said Ellis continued to recover from a side strain sustained during Australia's earlier South Africa tour. Dwarshuis was added to the T20I squad in his place. Meredith, meanwhile, joined the ODI squad. Saying “Dwarshuis replaced Beardman in Adelaide” would combine two distinct decisions and be wrong.

The separation matters because this tour has two formats. Three ODIs were scheduled first, followed by five T20 internationals. A player can be in one squad and not the other, and a replacement for a T20 absence is not automatically a replacement in an ODI XI. Readers who see a table of Australia–England fixtures should look at the format before interpreting a squad update. Ellis's continued recovery explains why the T20 side needed its own adjustment.

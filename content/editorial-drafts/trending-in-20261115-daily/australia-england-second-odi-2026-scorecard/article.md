## Australia vs England second ODI 2026: Adelaide result and scorecard

England beat Australia by 135 runs in the second one-day international at Adelaide Oval on 15 November 2026. England made 355/6 in 50 overs and Australia were all out for 220. Tom Banton's 121 from 99 balls anchored the visitors' innings, with significant support from Joe Root, Jordan Cox and Will Jacks. Gus Atkinson then took four wickets for 28 runs as Australia's chase lost four wickets in the first ten overs. The result levelled the three-match series at 1-1, with a decider scheduled for Hobart on 18 November. [Cricket Australia's completed live report](https://www.cricket.com.au/news/4608674/live-blog-australia-v-england-second-odi-perth-stadium-marsh-brook-beardman-root-archer-head-meredith) confirms the 135-run margin; the [completed scorecard](https://www.cricketworld.com/cricket/australia-vs-england/match/scorecard/96697) supplies the individual innings and bowling figures.

This page is about the finished Adelaide match. It is separate from a pre-match squad story about Riley Meredith's selection and from the first ODI in Perth. That distinction matters when search results show several “Australia vs England” pages at once. The Perth match gave Australia a 1-0 lead. Adelaide brought England back to parity. Hobart was the remaining fixture, not a replay of Adelaide. The teams' batting tables below are for the second ODI only, and the score is final rather than a snapshot taken during a live chase.

Banton's 121 was the central innings. The Cricket Australia highlights page also records the 121 from 99 balls, while Cricket World's report explains that he reached his hundred from 86 deliveries. His score was not an isolated late burst. He and Root stabilized England's innings after the first wicket and converted a good start into a platform for the lower middle order. Root's 62 ensured the partnership was not carried by one batter alone. Once both had contributed heavily, England could maintain pressure even after wickets fell.

The completed scorecard shows England at 54/1 after the opening ten overs. That start was solid rather than explosive. The innings expanded through the Banton–Root partnership, then Cox and Jacks produced the faster late scoring. Cox made 67 from 49 deliveries and Jacks remained unbeaten on 54 from 40. Cricket World's report says England scored 110 in the final ten overs. That is one reason 355 became possible: the side did not spend all of its resources simply reaching 250; it accelerated late with set batters and wickets in hand.

Atkinson's 13 not out from four balls also appears in the scorecard. It is a small innings by balls faced, yet it is part of the final total and shows the late intent. Readers should avoid using a highlight focused only on Banton to infer that he scored most of the runs without help. England's score depended on the hundred, Root's platform, Cox and Jacks closing strongly, extras and a few final contributions. The table makes those parts visible rather than turning the innings into a single-player headline.

### Australian bowling against England

Xavier Bartlett took three wickets but conceded 72 in ten overs. Spencer Johnson took one for 61, while Riley Meredith took one for 75 in ten. Cooper Connolly claimed Root's wicket in a shorter spell. The scorecard lists Adam Zampa without a wicket in his nine overs and Jack Edwards without a wicket in nine. Those figures explain why Australia's wicket-taking did not fully control England's run rate. Wickets can matter while the opposition still builds a imposing total, particularly when set batters score quickly between dismissals.

Meredith's wicket was England captain Harry Brook, bowled for five. That specific fact adds the actual on-field outcome to the separate pre-match selection story. Australia had brought Meredith in for Mahli Beardman, who was being managed after his six-wicket debut in Perth. The Adelaide scorecard confirms Meredith's ten overs and Brook's wicket. It does not show that one selection caused the eventual defeat; reducing a 135-run result to a single pace change would ignore England's innings and Australia's batting collapse.

Bartlett's three wickets likewise should not be read as an uncomplicated success story. He removed Duckett, Banton and Cox, but England still finished at 355. Bowling analysis needs both dismissals and runs conceded. A wicket at the end of a large partnership can prevent further runs, yet the batting side may already have a strong platform. Australia's task at the halfway mark was therefore still substantial, even though its bowlers had removed six batters by the end.

A chase of 356 already requires a sustained scoring rate over fifty overs; after losing four top-order players quickly, the batting side must also avoid another cluster of wickets. Renshaw and Green gave the innings some structure, but the recovery came at a slower pace than the target demanded. At that stage, merely keeping a partnership alive could not guarantee a competitive finish.

Renshaw's 63 from 74 balls and Green's 64 from 82 were valuable resistance. They lifted Australia far beyond the first powerplay collapse. Yet cricket scoring is relative to a target. A half-century can be the best innings on one side and still leave that team short when the opposition has already posted 355. Green's eventual dismissal came after Australia had lost more wickets, and later contributions from Zampa, Johnson and Meredith were unable to close the gap. The 135-run margin reflects both England's high total and its successful defence.

### How England bowled Australia out

Gus Atkinson's four wickets for 28 runs from ten overs stand out in the bowling table. He removed Head, Connolly and Inglis during Australia's early trouble and later dismissed Zampa. The economy of 2.8 runs per over was particularly significant in a chase that needed a fast scoring rate. He did not simply collect wickets while conceding freely; he restricted scoring across a full ten-over allocation. That dual effect placed pressure on the batters who survived.

Liam Dawson took two wickets, removing the two Australian half-centurions, Renshaw and Green. Sam Curran also claimed two, including consecutive wickets of Edwards and Bartlett in the scorecard sequence. Sonny Baker dismissed Marsh, and Jofra Archer bowled Johnson to end the innings. The distribution shows England's defence was a team effort. Atkinson provided the standout analysis, but other bowlers broke the middle-order recovery and finished the tail.

The fall of wickets records Australia at 13/3 and 29/4 before the Renshaw–Green recovery. These points are more informative than a single final total because they show the chase's changing prospects. At 29/4, Australia had used much of its top order while still needing hundreds of runs. At 127/5, it had stabilized somewhat but remained well behind. At 173/8, the chase had become a fight to extend the innings rather than a plausible pursuit of 356. Those are inferences from the score, not claims about private tactical discussions in either dressing room.

### Why Banton's hundred mattered after Perth

Australia had won the first ODI in Perth by seven wickets after Mahli Beardman's 6/24 helped dismiss England for 131. A second heavy batting failure could have left England 2-0 down with the series already lost. Instead, Banton built a hundred at Adelaide and the middle order added enough to reach 355/6. The 224-run difference between England's Perth and Adelaide totals is a simple measure of the batting response, though different venues, conditions and bowling attacks mean it should not be treated as a controlled experiment.

This contrast explains why the Adelaide result mattered beyond one scorecard. England levelled the series immediately and turned Hobart into a decider. Australia retained home advantage but no longer held a series lead. At the time this article was written, the third ODI was scheduled for 18 November; its result should not be guessed from the second match. A reader looking for a “series winner” after Adelaide should find the answer “not decided yet,” despite England's emphatic win on the day.

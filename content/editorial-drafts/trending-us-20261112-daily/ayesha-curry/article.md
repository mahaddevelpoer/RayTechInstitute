## How to discuss chemistry with more precision

Viewers often say that two performers have chemistry. The phrase describes an impression but does not explain its ingredients. A more useful discussion considers timing, attention, reactions and how the characters' choices affect one another. Those observations allow someone else to understand the basis of your response.

Try an original rehearsal exercise with two readers and a neutral exchange about a misplaced book. First have them deliver the lines without responding to each other's timing. Then let each pause or react naturally to what was just said. The words remain the same, but the interaction can feel different because the listening changes.

This does not prove how any professional pair worked on set. It gives students a vocabulary for discussing performance beyond a simple rating. You can describe an interruption, a withheld reply or a shift in attention without inventing a personal relationship between the performers.

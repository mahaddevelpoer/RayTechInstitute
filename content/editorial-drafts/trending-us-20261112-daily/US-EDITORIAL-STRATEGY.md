# US editorial selection and AdSense assessment

Assessment date: 12 November 2026.

The owner reports some US reach. No country-level AdSense earnings, Search Console clicks or analytics engagement report was available for this assessment. Do not describe the strategy as proven profitable or publish invented RPM comparisons.

## Recommendation

Continue US-facing content selectively, prioritising the site's educational purpose: programming, practical technology, digital literacy and accessible science. US geography can be commercially relevant, but the topic's country and the actual visitor's country are different facts. An article about the United States does not itself create US ad impressions.

The previous broad selection mixed public-safety reports, celebrity profiles, sports and educational explainers. That is a weaker editorial focus for this institution than a recognisable set of useful learning topics. This is an editorial judgement, not a claim that every general-interest site is penalised or that an unrelated page automatically harms every other page.

Do not delete, redirect or change indexing of existing published articles as part of this recommendation without evaluating the affected pages. No such destructive action was performed.

## Evidence and limits

Google says AdSense earnings depend on factors including traffic, content, user location and ad setup. A universally high US RPM is not promised. A small amount of US traffic is encouraging, but is not enough on its own to establish a good return on editorial effort.

Google Search guidance asks whether a site has a primary purpose and warns against producing content on many topics merely because they are trending. It also explicitly states that Google has no preferred word count. The owner's current 2,000-word editorial requirement remains in effect for this queue, but length and tag count must not be described as ranking or income guarantees. Add useful explanation rather than padding.

## Measure the experiment

Use a common review period and keep these measures distinct:

- Search Console: US-filtered clicks, impressions, queries and landing pages. Impressions are not site visits.
- Analytics: actual US users or sessions, landing pages, engagement and returning visits. Do not equate sessions with page views.
- AdSense, if approved and serving: country-level page views, estimated earnings and Page RPM, using comparable filters and dates.
- Editorial effort: writing, research, image review and maintenance time per article or topic cluster.

Page RPM = estimated earnings divided by page views, multiplied by 1,000. Revenue implied by a stated Page RPM = page views divided by 1,000, multiplied by that RPM.

A purely hypothetical example: 10,000 page views at a $5 Page RPM corresponds to $50; at $15 it corresponds to $150. These are arithmetic examples, not US market benchmarks, a forecast or a claim about this website's actual performance.

Review a consistent several-week window rather than treating one newly indexed page or one day of impressions as a reliable long-term result. This is a recommendation, not an automated monitoring task. Prioritise topic clusters that bring relevant engaged readers and useful revenue relative to their effort. Where ads are not yet serving, do not invent earnings from search impressions.

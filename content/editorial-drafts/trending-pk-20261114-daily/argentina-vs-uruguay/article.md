## Argentina 1-2 Uruguay: the November 2026 friendly in Buenos Aires

### Why the first goal is recorded as an own goal

The first score change came after half-time, and the official AUF record labels it 'OG' beside Argentina defender Leonardo Balerdi. That label matters. A quick summary might claim that an Uruguay forward opened the scoring, but both federations identify a Balerdi own goal. The goal gave Uruguay the lead without adding a goal to any Uruguayan player's personal tally.

Own goals often cause confusion in scorecards. The team receiving the benefit is awarded a goal in the match score, while the player who last put the ball into his own net is named in the event description. The AUF card's 48th-minute entry therefore answers two different questions: Uruguay went ahead, and Balerdi was the player associated with the own goal. It does not identify the whole preceding attacking move, so this account does not invent one.

The first half had ended 0-0. That makes the second-half opening especially important: the stalemate ended quickly after the interval, then changed again just five minutes later. The score at 48 minutes was Argentina 0, Uruguay 1. A reader who only sees the final 1-2 may miss how tightly the decisive events were clustered after half-time.

The scoreboard became Argentina 1, Uruguay 2, leaving Argentina a route to an equaliser. The federations' published accounts do not list a later goal, so the match ended 2-1 to Uruguay.

Álvarez had started for Argentina. The team sheet also lists Nico Paz, Thiago Almada, Enzo Fernández and Alexis Mac Allister among the starters. ### Match date in Argentina and Pakistan

The official AUF fixture lists kick-off at 20:00 local time on 13 November. Buenos Aires is eight hours behind Pakistan Standard Time during November, so that start falls at 04:00 on 14 November in Pakistan. The game and its post-match discussion could therefore trend in Pakistan on 14 November without any conflict in the match date. This is why both dates may appear in searches and why this page places the local official date in its headline and match table.

Time zones create a similar issue in international cricket, football and other events. A source published after midnight in one country can cover a match that began on the previous calendar day elsewhere. The remedy is to say whether a date is the venue's local date, a viewer's local date, or a publication date. The AUF record is explicit about its local kick-off. That is more reliable than treating a search-trend timestamp as a fixture date.

In practical terms, a Pakistan reader looking for a '14 November Argentina Uruguay score' should still open the 13 November fixture. The teams, 1-2 result and La Bombonera venue identify it.

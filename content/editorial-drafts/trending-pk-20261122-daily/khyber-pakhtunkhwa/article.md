**Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, often abbreviated KP, is a province of Pakistan whose capital is Peshawar.** A place name from the province can identify a city, district, division or a much smaller statistical area. Knowing which level a source means is essential when reading a map, a census table or an education-related news report.

This guide uses provincial government material and Pakistan Bureau of Statistics resources checked on 23 November 2026. Its main worked example is PBS's Census 2023 geographic frame. The frame's charges, circles and blocks are census-operation units; their counts must not be presented as numbers of districts or current population figures.

## How are the province and Peshawar related?

Peshawar is the provincial capital, while Khyber Pakhtunkhwa is the larger provincial unit. The two names therefore cannot substitute for each other in every sentence. A measurement made in Peshawar does not automatically describe the whole province.

Provincial government material identifies Peshawar as the capital, and PBS publishes separate national, provincial and district census resources. Those sources help establish the level of a claim before a reader tries to compare its numerical value with another table.

For a school notebook, use separate fields for Province, District or other named area, and Site. A photograph of a building can be assigned to a site if its source establishes the location. A provincial statistic belongs in a different field and needs a source that covers the province.

This approach prevents a common shortcut in project reports: taking an attractive photograph from one city and writing a caption that implies it measures conditions everywhere. Images can illustrate a location, but a province-wide conclusion requires evidence collected or compiled at the relevant level.

## Why do administrative and statistical units need different labels?

An administrative district and a census block serve different purposes. One is a unit within an administrative structure; the other belongs to the organisation of statistical enumeration. Their boundaries may relate to one another, but the labels are not interchangeable.

PBS's geographic-information material describes the preparation and subdivision of enumeration blocks for Census 2023. A table of these blocks tells readers about the statistical frame. It is not a new list of provinces, cities or elected constituencies.

When copying a number, copy its column heading as well. A value separated from its heading can acquire a completely different meaning in a social-media caption. The arithmetic may remain unchanged while the claim becomes wrong because the unit has been relabelled.

Create a glossary before starting a geography assignment. Include Province, District, Division, Tehsil and Census block where the source uses those terms. Add the source's own definitions or direct readers to them. Do not assume that every hierarchy or reporting table uses the same set of levels.

## What does PBS's Census 2023 frame show for KP?

The PBS GIS table provides the following counts for Khyber Pakhtunkhwa in its Census 2023 frame. They are reproduced here as a source-reading example, with the rural and urban columns kept separate.

| Census-frame unit | Rural | Urban | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Charges | 331 | 79 | 410 |
| Circles | 2,307 | 470 | 2,777 |
| Blocks | 24,988 | 3,885 | 28,873 |

Each row counts a different kind of unit. The total number of charges is not supposed to equal the total number of blocks. Comparing the rows as though they were three estimates of one quantity would misunderstand the structure of the table.

The reference label Census 2023 is also important. These are counts in the identified published frame, not a statement that no boundary, classification or operational arrangement could ever change afterward. A current administrative question requires the appropriate current notice.

## How can the table's arithmetic be checked?

The within-row calculations are straightforward: **331 + 79 = 410**, **2,307 + 470 = 2,777**, and **24,988 + 3,885 = 28,873**. These checks confirm that each displayed total matches the two displayed components.

They do not independently verify every boundary or the completeness of the underlying frame. An arithmetic consistency check and a full statistical validation are different activities. A student should explain which one they performed rather than calling a checked sum an audit of an entire census.

Next inspect the units. It is meaningful to add rural blocks to urban blocks in the same table. It is not meaningful to add blocks to circles and describe the result as a count of districts. The categories determine which operation answers the question.

In a spreadsheet, use separate columns for Unit type, Rural, Urban and Total. Include a formula that checks whether Rural plus Urban equals Total for each row. A visible zero difference is useful for checking transcription, but the headings remain necessary to interpret the result correctly.

## Does the share of blocks equal the share of population?

No population share can be established from a block count alone. A block is a reporting or enumeration unit, not a fixed number of residents. Different blocks can contain different numbers of people, so the proportion of blocks and the proportion of population are distinct measurements.

In the displayed frame, the urban-block share is approximately **3,885 ÷ 28,873 × 100 = 13.46 percent**. That statement concerns blocks in this table. It must not be rewritten as saying that 13.46 percent of KP's population is urban.

To calculate an urban-population share, obtain the matching urban and total population values from a population table using the same stated coverage and reference period. Then identify any scope notes. The denominator should count people if the question asks about people.

A useful classroom comparison asks students to explain why the two percentages might differ without treating the difference as a mistake. The exercise tests whether they understand the unit being counted, rather than only whether they can enter two numbers into a calculator.

## What does a census reference year tell the reader?

The reference year identifies the observation or census period to which the material relates. The publication date can be later, and a website's update date can be later still. Those dates serve different purposes and should be recorded separately.

PBS's release page explains that detailed Census 2023 national and provincial reports were published as separate resources. A report appearing on a later website date can still describe Census 2023. Calling its values a new 2026 population count merely because it was accessed in 2026 would be inaccurate.

For a source record, write Reference period, Report edition, Publication date where available and Access date. If one of those fields is not established, leave it unconfirmed. A precise-looking invented date is less useful than an honest statement of the available information.

This method also helps when two students consult different editions. They can compare publication records and scope notes before deciding whether a value changed through revision, a changed definition or an ordinary copying error. The source history becomes part of the explanation.

## Why can two official tables show different totals?

Before treating different totals as a contradiction, check their scope. Tables can differ in included areas, available characteristics, special enumeration arrangements, reference versions or the unit being measured. The label Official does not mean that every table answers exactly the same question.

For example, a table compiled around a particular demographic characteristic may carry notes about coverage that a broad headline total does not share. The correct response is to inspect those notes and the table's denominator, not to silently choose whichever number best fits a desired conclusion.

This guide deliberately does not construct a current population headline by mixing figures from separate PBS tables. Its numerical example stays within one identified GIS frame, where the unit headings and row totals can be checked directly.

When an assignment needs a population figure, identify the exact report, table, page and scope used. If an apparent discrepancy remains unresolved, describe it as unresolved and retain both references. Do not invent a reconciliation or imply that matching logos make different definitions identical.

## How should divisions, districts and cities be compared?

A larger administrative or statistical area can contain several smaller areas. A value for the larger area cannot automatically be assigned to one of its cities. Likewise, adding city values does not necessarily reconstruct a larger-area total if the cities do not cover every locality included in it.

Imagine a fictional district containing two named urban centres and additional rural localities. A table listing only the two centres would not cover the district's entire population. The missing rural component is a scope issue, not a mathematical mystery.

When joining tables, identify whether each row represents an exhaustive subdivision of the parent area. If it does not, explain the uncovered part. Do not use a partial list to calculate a supposedly complete provincial total.

A practical worksheet can provide a made-up hierarchy and ask students to select the rows needed for a specified total. This teaches geographic reasoning without using outdated district counts or pretending that a familiar place name defines its own boundary in every dataset.

## How can counts and service rates give different impressions?

Suppose two fictional areas report school libraries. Area A has eight libraries for 20,000 residents, while Area B has twenty for 100,000. A has **4 libraries per 10,000 residents**, and B has **2 per 10,000**, despite B's larger absolute count.

These are invented teaching values, not measurements of KP's education services. The example shows how a denominator changes the question. A facility count describes the number of facilities; a population-based rate describes their number relative to residents under the stated assumptions.

Neither indicator, by itself, establishes library quality, opening hours, student access or whether facilities are evenly distributed. A careful report can use the rate to identify a question while acknowledging that further information is needed to assess the service.

If real data are used, align the reference year, area boundary and definition of a library. A school reading room and a public library may be separate categories. Combining them without explanation could make the calculation look more precise while weakening its meaning.

## What can a landscape photograph establish?

A properly sourced image can illustrate the location and subject identified by its caption. It can help students recognise terrain or architecture and make a geography lesson easier to follow. It does not independently establish an economic indicator, a population trend or an administrative boundary.

For a mountain photograph, identify the documented location and avoid assigning a whole province's weather to one visible scene. A photograph records a particular view under particular conditions. The wider geographic question may require maps, observations across time or another dataset.

For a building photograph, distinguish architectural context from evidence about the work of an institution. An image of a museum or administrative building does not prove that an application was approved or a public service is currently available.

Good captions state what the image depicts and disclose when it is illustrative. Our article uses credited images for geographic context; the linked official sources establish the administrative and statistical statements. Those sources perform different roles and should remain distinguishable to the reader.

## How can students build a source-based KP study sheet?

Start with the province and capital, then add a short glossary of geographic levels. Include the Census 2023 frame table with its unit labels and reference. Finish with one arithmetic check and a paragraph explaining why block shares are not population shares.

Use our [province introduction](/blogs/what-is-a-province-pakistan-federation-districts-guide/) for the wider distinction between provinces, districts and cities. Use the KP-specific official material below for the particular source record examined in this article.

For the final exercise, give a classmate an unlabeled number from your table and ask what information is missing. The correct response should request the unit, geographic coverage, reference period and source, rather than guess what the number measures.

This is the central learning skill: a place name locates a subject, but a reliable geographic claim also needs a defined area, an identified quantity and an appropriate source. Those details make a KP report clearer and prevent a local observation from becoming an unsupported provincial claim.

## A check on what a block count cannot answer

Imagine two fictional blocks, each counted as one unit in a frame. One contains 80 households and the other 120. The frame contains two blocks and 200 households under these supplied values. The equal block counts do not imply equal household counts.

Now suppose a student receives only the number of blocks. They cannot recover the household total without additional information about the blocks. Applying an assumed average would create an estimate that needs to be labelled and justified, rather than a directly observed count.

This example explains why the KP frame table should be used for its stated purpose. It can support a comparison of enumeration units. A question about residents, households or facilities requires the corresponding observations and definitions.

Nitish Kumar Reddy's all-round role is best understood by looking at batting and bowling separately before combining them. A player can contribute in both disciplines without having the same responsibility in every match. Identify the team, format and fixture before comparing an international appearance with a franchise performance.

## Batting position changes the task

An opener begins against a new ball, while a middle-order batter may arrive after early wickets or during a planned acceleration. Record the entry score, overs remaining and target. A quick twenty and a patient fifty can each be valuable in different circumstances; the final total alone cannot establish their importance.

Read strike rate alongside balls faced and the match situation. A short innings produces a volatile percentage. Compare several similar appearances before describing a lasting change in technique or intent.

## Bowling workload matters

An all-rounder may bowl a full allocation, a short supporting spell or no overs at all. Team balance, conditions and available specialist bowlers affect that decision. Not bowling in one game is not sufficient evidence of injury or a permanent change of role.

For each spell, record overs, runs and wickets. Then note the phase: a powerplay over and a late-innings over involve different field restrictions and batter intentions. Use legal deliveries correctly when calculating an economy rate.

## Keep records in their own categories

Test, ODI, T20I and domestic T20 statistics should remain distinct. A franchise contract or squad selection also needs a dated official source. Search snippets from previous seasons can persist after teams change, so the linked board and league directories are starting points rather than proof of a current line-up.

## Build an honest comparison

Choose a defined sample of completed matches and create columns for batting entry, runs, balls, bowling allocation and fielding contribution. Explain missing opportunities instead of treating every blank as failure. Finish with one strength supported by evidence and one question requiring a larger sample.

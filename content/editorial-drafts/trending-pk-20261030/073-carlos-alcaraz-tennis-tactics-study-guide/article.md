Carlos Alcaraz's ATP and Spanish federation profiles provide official starting points for his tennis record. To learn from a match, look at how a player creates and uses court space. Attacking is not only hitting harder; placement, timing and the opponent's position can make a slower shot the more demanding option.

## Follow court position

Observe where each player stands when the rally begins and how that position changes. A deep shot can push an opponent back, while a shorter angle can pull them wide. The next available shot depends on that movement, not just on the quality of the final winner.

A highlight often begins near the dramatic finish. Watch a complete rally to see how the advantage was built and whether an earlier defensive shot kept the point alive.

## Understand a change of pace

A drop shot can exploit an opponent positioned far behind the baseline, but it also needs control and an appropriate situation. A lob, slice or higher ball can alter rhythm. Judge the choice against the available space rather than calling every successful variation a surprise.

Surface and conditions influence bounce and movement. A tactic that works in one event may need adjustment in another. Explain the setting when comparing matches.

## Read the score alongside the rally

Break points, service games and tie-breaks create different pressures. A player may accept more risk while trailing, but the score does not make every attacking option automatically correct. Separate the decision from the eventual outcome.

Rankings, titles and tournament entries change. Use a dated official record rather than an old snippet, and avoid turning a withdrawal into an unsupported health diagnosis.

## A practical viewing task

Choose three complete points: one attacking, one defensive and one involving a change of pace. Draw the players' positions and explain the opening each shot created.

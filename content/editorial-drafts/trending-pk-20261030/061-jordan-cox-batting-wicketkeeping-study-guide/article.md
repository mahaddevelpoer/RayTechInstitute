A batting role, wicketkeeping duty and squad place are related but separate facts. To analyse a performance, establish what the player actually did in that match before judging the numbers.

## Identify the competition and season

County cricket, franchise tournaments and international matches belong to different records. A club performance should not be added to an England total. Check the season and team on the scorecard, especially when a highlight clip uses only a surname.

Squad membership does not mean a player started every fixture. Likewise, a profile describing someone as a wicketkeeper does not establish that they kept wicket in the match you are studying.

## Read the batting situation

Record the entry score, target and overs available. Top-order and finishing roles place different demands on shot selection. Runs, balls faced and strike rate become more informative when the team situation is visible.

Observe strike rotation between boundaries. An edited clip can make an innings look like uninterrupted hitting while omitting quiet deliveries, missed opportunities and partnership management. Use the completed scorecard to restore the broader picture.

## Add the fielding responsibility

Where the player keeps wicket, consider collection, footwork and the chances offered. Catches and stumpings are recorded outcomes, but a simple total does not show every clean take or saved run. Avoid inventing a numerical contribution for an action that was not measured.

## Compare with a defined sample

Choose several appearances in the same format and identify a repeated pattern. One excellent innings can demonstrate capability without proving consistency; one poor score cannot establish a permanent weakness. Current selection, injury and contract claims need a dated official announcement.

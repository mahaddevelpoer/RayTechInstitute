## What does finishing require?

In a chase, the batter must compare runs needed with legal deliveries remaining. When setting a total, the task is different: there is no final target yet, and the value of keeping a wicket can change with the remaining overs. These situations should not be treated as identical.

Watch the available scoring areas and the field. A boundary option may involve more risk than a single, but the required rate can make that risk necessary. A missed shot is not automatically a bad decision; explain the delivery and match situation.

## Partnerships and strike matter

The batter at the other end affects how an over is managed. Taking a single can preserve a partnership or move the more suitable player onto strike. Avoid assuming that refusing or accepting a run is always right without looking at both players' roles.

Dot balls and extras can change the equation quickly. Record the score after each legal delivery to understand why pressure rises even when the required number of boundaries looks small.

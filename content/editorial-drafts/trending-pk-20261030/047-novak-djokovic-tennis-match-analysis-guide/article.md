Novak Djokovic's official ITF profile is a starting point for player identification and competition records. To understand a tennis performance, first follow points, games and sets. A dramatic rally can be memorable without explaining why one player controlled the match or how the score developed.

## Keep the scoring layers separate

Points build a game, games build a set and sets decide a match under the event's format. A break of serve means the receiving player wins a service game. It does not mean the match is over, and its importance depends on where it occurs in the set.

Tie-break rules and the number of sets can vary by competition. Check the event's regulations rather than applying one tournament's format to every match.

## Watch the return game

Observe return position, depth and the server's second-shot options. A deep return can reduce attacking space even without producing an immediate winner. First-serve percentage, points won behind serve and break-point opportunities answer different questions.

One successful return does not establish a complete tactical pattern. Follow several games and note whether the opponent adjusts placement or pace.

## Rally patterns need context

Surface, conditions and the score influence shot selection. A defensive recovery can change the balance of a point without ending it. Compare court position and risk, not only the number of winners in a highlights package.

Avoid turning visible fatigue into an unsupported medical claim. Injuries and withdrawals require reliable announcements, while a difficult passage of play can have several explanations.

## Verify records before sharing them

Rankings, title totals and upcoming entries change. Use a dated official profile or tournament record instead of an old search snippet. This draft does not freeze a current ranking or claim a new title.

Super Six usually describes a tournament stage involving six qualifying teams. The name alone does not tell you which results count, how many matches remain or who advances. Read the competition's own playing conditions before calculating qualification scenarios, because formats with the same label can operate differently.

## Identify the tournament first

A World Cup qualifier, an age-group event and a regional competition may use different structures. Confirm the edition and season. A diagram from an older tournament is not automatically valid for a new one, even where the stage has a familiar name.

Make a simple bracket showing the first round, the qualifying teams and the next stage. This separates the route to qualification from the table used after teams arrive there.

## Carried points need careful reading

Some formats carry forward selected earlier results; others start a fresh table. Where carry-over applies, find out which opponents' results count and whether the related scoring data also carries. Do not add every group-stage point simply because a team earned it earlier.

For an illustrative exercise, suppose a team brings two points into a later stage and earns another two. Its resulting four points come from the specified rules, not from a universal Super Six formula. The example is not a current standings claim.

## Net run rate is a separate calculation

Net run rate uses scoring and conceding rates under the tournament's rules. It is not just runs scored minus runs conceded. All-out innings, reduced matches and revised targets may require particular treatment. Use the official table for the published figure and the conditions for the method.

## Evaluate qualification scenarios honestly

Separate mathematical possibility from likelihood. A team may need both its own win and another match's outcome. An unfinished fixture means the scenario is not settled. Explain assumptions and avoid declaring qualification before the relevant result is confirmed.

Students can practise by making a small table with played, points, remaining fixtures and tie-break rules. Our [standings guide](/blogs/al-nassr-al-kholood-standings-explained/) explains why games played and points must be read together, although football and cricket use their own rules.

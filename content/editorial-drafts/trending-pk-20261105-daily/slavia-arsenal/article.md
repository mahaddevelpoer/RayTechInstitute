## Why total shots do not determine the winner

A shot from a difficult angle and a close-range opportunity both add one to a basic shot count, but they are not identical scoring situations. Blocks, misses and saves also differ. Use the categories provided by the data source and avoid treating every attempt as an equally dangerous chance.

A match centre can show one side making more attempts while the other scores more goals. That describes a difference between attempt volume and conversion, not a broken scoreboard. To understand it, inspect the locations and circumstances rather than simply assuming the side with more shots deserved a particular result.

Do not invent an efficiency explanation from the total alone. More goals from fewer attempts can reflect several factors, including opportunity quality and finishing. Goalkeeping and defensive blocks matter too. A careful review can suggest questions without claiming that an aggregate number reveals the cause with certainty.

## Expected goals is a model, not an alternative result

Expected goals assigns probabilities to shots using a model's features and data. It is useful for discussing opportunity quality, but it is not a second scoreboard. A model estimate does not replace the goals awarded during the game or change which side receives the points.

Different services can produce different estimates because their models and inputs differ. Keep the provider named and use a consistent source when comparing matches. Avoid presenting a decimal as an official determination of what a team should certainly have scored.

Suppose an imaginary chance has a model probability of 0.20. That does not mean the next five identical-looking shots must produce exactly one goal. It describes an estimated likelihood across comparable situations. Real matches contain variation, and a short sequence can differ substantially from the model average.

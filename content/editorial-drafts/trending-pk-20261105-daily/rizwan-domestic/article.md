## What does 200 not out actually mean?

An unbeaten double century means the batter reached at least 200 runs and had not been dismissed when that innings ended. In this case the team declaration ended the innings. The asterisk commonly printed after 200 records the unbeaten status; it does not add another run, indicate a bonus or describe an unfinished personal milestone.

Compare two invented scorecards. A batter scoring 200 before being caught contributes the same number of team runs as someone finishing on 200 not out. However, the dismissal status differs. That difference matters when aggregating a batting average, because the denominator is dismissals rather than appearances or total innings. It does not mean the unbeaten player faced no difficult chances.

Nor can a scorecard alone tell us that the innings was flawless. Dropped chances, the quality of individual deliveries, field settings and conditions require additional evidence. A responsible description can praise the size and duration of the contribution without inventing a ball-by-ball account that the published figures do not provide.

## Read the boundary figures as a small maths lesson

The reported boundary count offers a straightforward calculation. Thirty-five fours contribute 140 runs. One six adds another six, so those boundaries account for 146 of the 200 runs. Subtracting gives 54 runs not accounted for by those particular boundary hits. That remainder is arithmetic, not a reconstructed sequence of singles, twos and threes.

We cannot divide those 54 runs into specific running patterns without a detailed record. For example, saying there were exactly 54 singles would be an invention. Overthrows and other scoring circumstances also need proper attribution. The safe conclusion is simply that the listed fours and six explain 73 percent of his individual total: 146 divided by 200, multiplied by 100.

This calculation adds context without creating a new match fact. It tells a reader that boundaries formed a substantial share of the innings. It does not tell us which bowler conceded them, whether they arrived against an old ball or a new ball, or which side of the ground was targeted most often.

## How fast was the innings?

Runs divided by balls faced, multiplied by 100, gives batting strike rate. Using 200 and 252 produces approximately 79.37 runs per 100 balls. That is a calculation from the reported figures, not a comparison with an unverified season ranking. It can be reproduced with an ordinary calculator.

Do not confuse this with a team's runs per over. A batter's strike rate measures personal scoring relative to balls faced. Team run rate uses the team's total and overs. The two statistics answer different questions, and neither alone proves the quality of an innings. A long-format side may value occupation of the crease as well as acceleration.

Imagine two fictional innings: 80 from 80 balls and 120 from 240. The first has a higher strike rate, but the second supplies more runs and consumes more deliveries. Depending on the match situation, either could be valuable. An evaluation needs the team's position, time remaining and opposition response, not just one attractive percentage.

## Why 115.2 overs is not a decimal

Cricket's notation for overs counts completed overs and additional legal balls. In a six-ball over, 115.2 means 115 completed overs plus two legal deliveries. It does not mean 115.2 ordinary decimal units. Treating the notation as a calculator decimal produces a small but avoidable error in derived run rates.

The conversion is 115 multiplied by six, plus two: 692 legal deliveries. Divide that by six to obtain 115.333 recurring overs for arithmetic. Then 503 divided by that quantity gives a team scoring rate of roughly 4.36 runs per over. This is an illustrative calculation using the published declaration figures.

The same technique works elsewhere in a scorecard. Ten overs and five balls is 65 legal balls, not 10.5 decimal overs. Practice converting the notation before comparing bowling economy or team scoring speed. A notation system can look familiar while using a different base; cricket is a useful everyday example of that problem.

## What did declaring on 503 for four mean?

A declaration is a captain's decision to close the team's innings rather than continue batting until all available dismissals occur. It trades the chance of more runs for time in which to bowl at the opposition. The team is considering the match, not merely the opportunity for individual statistics.

The published declaration does not by itself reveal every reason behind the timing. Conditions, remaining playing time, weather and bowling resources can all affect such decisions, but attributing a particular motive needs a captain's explanation or reliable match reporting. It is enough here to observe that Peshawar ended the innings with wickets still available.

For learners, distinguish the notation carefully. A score of 503 for four declared is not the same as 503 all out. Both can leave the opponent needing a large first-innings response, but the remaining wickets tell different stories about how the innings ended. A headline that drops the declaration can hide that strategic choice.

A double century is an individual achievement within a team contest. To establish the match outcome, a reader must also examine the other innings and the final result line. ## Does this guarantee an international recall?

No individual domestic innings guarantees selection. A selection announcement is a separate record produced by the relevant board. A score can contribute to an assessment of form, but availability, team balance, role requirements and the selectors' published decision remain different questions. Do not convert a sporting achievement into an appointment that has not been announced.

This is not a judgement against the performance. It is a distinction between evidence and prediction. The scorecard establishes runs, balls and dismissal status. A squad release establishes whether a player has been selected. A journalist's forecast or a supporter's preference can discuss possibilities, but should be labelled accordingly instead of presented as official confirmation.

For students practising sports writing, prepare two separate sentences. The first reports the verified innings. The second, if necessary, says that a future selection decision remains for the board. This avoids sensational language while retaining the significance of a substantial contribution in a recognised domestic competition.

## How to compare this innings with another domestic performance

Use a comparison sheet with columns for format, opponent, venue, innings position, runs, balls and dismissal status. Add the team total and final match result only when available. That structure prevents a limited-overs cameo from being compared with a first-class double century as if the two were attempts at an identical task.

Do not manufacture a career record from one report. Claims about a fastest double century, a ground record or the longest gap between milestones need historical data. An impressive number may tempt a writer to add a superlative, but the superlative is a separate claim. It deserves its own evidence and clearly defined scope.

A useful comparison can still be modest. Calculate the boundary share and strike rate for each innings using the same method, then explain what those numbers omit. Students can learn more from a transparent calculation than from an unsupported declaration that an innings was the greatest or most important of a player's career.

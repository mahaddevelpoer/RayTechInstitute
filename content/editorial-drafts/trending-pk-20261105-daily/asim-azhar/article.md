## A tour listing and a ticket offer are different records

The tour listing identifies an announced appearance. A ticket offer describes a particular transaction, including category, price and conditions. A show can be listed while a specific category is unavailable. Likewise, a third-party seller may advertise an offer that does not match the official date or venue.

Before comparing prices, make sure the products are comparable. A general-admission entry, a reserved seat and a package with additional benefits are not automatically identical. Read what is included and what is excluded. The lowest visible number can be misleading if it belongs to a different category or omits a required charge.

This article deliberately leaves price fields unfilled. We have not verified a current payable amount for each event. A made-up ticket price would not help a reader plan accurately. Use the actual checkout information and keep the total, category and date together when deciding whether an offer fits your budget.

## Check the total, not just the headline amount

A ticket checkout can contain a base price and additional components. The meaningful comparison is the total for the selected quantity and category under the stated terms. Do not assume that two posters with the same large number necessarily produce the same final payment.

For an original fictional example, imagine two tickets at 2,000 units each plus a 300-unit order charge. The total would be 4,300 units. If another offer includes the charge in a 2,100-unit per-ticket price, two tickets would total 4,200. These are arithmetic examples, not prices for Asim Azhar's tour.

The lesson is to write the calculation before paying: quantity multiplied by the applicable price, then add only the stated charges. Check whether a fee applies per ticket or per order. That small distinction can change a group purchase, and a clear total is more useful than guessing from a cropped promotional image.

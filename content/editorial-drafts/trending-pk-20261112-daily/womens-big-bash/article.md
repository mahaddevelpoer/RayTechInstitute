## How to read a fixture page

A fixture describes a planned match. A result describes a completed contest. A match centre may move between upcoming, live and completed states as the event develops. A search engine's saved excerpt can lag behind that change.

If a page still says upcoming after another source reports a result, check its update context rather than immediately copying whichever line appears first. The official current match record should be the reference for a completed score.

Also distinguish an event listing from a ticket offer. A ground's programme can establish that a fixture is planned without guaranteeing a particular seat or price. Read the actual booking terms before making attendance arrangements.

## T20 cricket creates several different phases

Although a T20 innings is relatively short, its early, middle and late periods can ask different questions of players. A side may need to recover from wickets, increase the rate or defend a particular boundary. The task changes with the situation.

A batter scoring steadily while wickets fall can be doing a different job from one entering with a strong platform near the finish. A bowler operating with a new ball may have a different plan from a teammate defending the final overs.

This does not mean every slow start is wise or every aggressive shot is justified. It means that a useful judgement describes the task before evaluating the decision. A raw total or strike rate becomes more informative when the match context is visible.

## A squad is not the playing eleven

A club's season squad identifies a group of players. It does not confirm that every member appears in a particular match. The selected eleven, substitutes where applicable and completed scorecard need their own checking.

An overseas signing can attract attention, but a promotional announcement does not establish participation in every fixture. Availability and team balance can change, so match-specific claims should use match-specific evidence.

For the same reason, an archive photograph of a well-known player is not proof of a new selection. Captions should identify the photograph's context and avoid attaching a current match result to an unrelated image.

## Batting average and runs per innings

Batting average uses runs divided by dismissals, not simply the number of innings. An explicitly fictional example illustrates the difference: a player scores 20, 40 and 30, with one innings ending not out. The total is 90, but there are two dismissals, giving an average of 45.

Dividing by all three innings gives 30 runs per innings, which is a different measure. Both calculations can be useful if labelled, but one should not be presented as the other.

The not-out information therefore matters when reading a scorecard. A short graphic containing only runs can be insufficient for reproducing an average. Keep the original inputs available rather than publishing a result whose denominator cannot be checked.

## Strike rate answers another question

Batting strike rate describes runs per 100 balls. For a fictional 30 from 25 deliveries, the calculation is 30 divided by 25, multiplied by 100, giving 120. It is a rate, not the same thing as an average or a team's scoring rate per over.

A high strike rate across very few balls needs a visible sample size. Ten from five and 60 from 30 share a rate of 200, but they are different contributions. The number should not hide the size of the innings.

When comparing players, keep the period and competition consistent. A career value, a season value and a single innings are not interchangeable even when they appear in similar boxes on a statistics page.

## Bowling figures contain several measures

Consider a fictional four-over spell with two wickets for 28 runs. The economy rate is seven runs per over. The bowling average for that spell is 14 runs per wicket. With 24 legal deliveries and two wickets, the strike rate is 12 balls per wicket.

The three numbers answer different questions about cost and wicket-taking frequency. None should be labelled as a complete assessment of the bowler without context, and the fictional example should not be attached to a real WBBL player.

Also check whether the figure describes an innings, a season or a career. A short excellent spell can move a small-sample average substantially. Keeping the sample visible makes comparisons fairer and reduces the temptation to declare a permanent conclusion after one match.

## Read the team score before judging one player

A player's contribution occurs within a team's task. The same innings can have a different significance in a low-scoring contest and a very high-scoring one. Likewise, an economical spell may be particularly valuable when an opponent is trying to accelerate.

That does not make every performance impossible to evaluate. It suggests a clearer order: establish the target or first-innings situation, identify the player's role and then consider the contribution.

For a learning exercise, write one sentence describing the team's problem before writing a sentence about the player. The habit forces the evaluation to connect with the match rather than rely only on reputation or a favourite highlight.

## Why the first-half lead mattered

A two-goal lead creates a different scoreboard problem from a one-goal lead. At 0-1, a single equaliser can bring the teams level. At 0-2, one reply still leaves the trailing team behind. That simple difference helps explain why the second first-half goal is significant without needing to invent a tactical change.

Consider the match from the perspective of a reader arriving at half-time. The correct immediate questions are how many goals separate the teams and what the next goal would change. An Al Ittihad goal would reduce the gap; it would not complete a comeback. An Al Ain goal would increase the task facing the home side.

This way of reading a score is more precise than describing every period as total control. A team can lead while facing pressure, or trail while creating opportunities. The available official summary establishes the lead and the final result. It does not, on its own, justify claims about domination throughout all ninety minutes.

## How to read the own goal

An own-goal entry identifies a scoring event credited against a player of the defending side. The goal increases the opposition's team total even though the named player belongs to the team conceding it. That is why Simic's name appears in the timeline while Al Ain's score rises from two to three.

## What does the official PBTE document confirm?

PBTE's circular is headed as an examination schedule for the **two-year Diploma in Information Technology, Second Annual Examination 2026**. It is dated 03-11-2026 and addressed to principals of affiliated government and private institutions. The commencement row states **04-01-2027**.

An examination cycle labelled 2026 can therefore begin on a calendar date in January 2027. Students should preserve the board's exact cycle name instead of renaming it First Annual 2027 or assuming it belongs to a different session. The cycle label matters when an institution selects admissions, checks eligibility and later retrieves results.

The circular is an **admission and fee schedule**, not a subject-by-subject date sheet. It does not, by itself, tell a student which subject will be examined first, the reporting time for each paper, or their individual examination centre. Those details need the relevant date sheet and candidate documents.

## Why are Rs 3,850 and Rs 3,700 both mentioned?

**Rs 3,850 is the amount in the supplied notice. Rs 3,700 is the single-fee total in the official PBTE circular. The difference is Rs 150.** The notice does not explain that difference, so this article does not label it an authorised college charge, a service fee, a printing fee or an error.

The board's single-fee total is itemised as follows. These are the figures visible in the circular for its stated examination category, not a personalised invoice for every student:

| PBTE single-fee component | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Theory examination fee | Rs 1,560 |
| Practical examination fee | Rs 580 |
| Data or assessment-related component shown in the circular | Rs 970 |
| Result card and postal charges | Rs 540 |
| CCTV camera charges | Rs 50 |
| **Total** | **Rs 3,700** |

The arithmetic is 1,560 + 580 + 970 + 540 + 50 = 3,700. A student asked to deposit 3,850 should request a written breakdown and a receipt. That is more useful than assuming the difference is automatically legitimate or accusing the institution of an error without first checking its explanation.

## Does 17 November replace the board's December deadline?

The supplied notice gives **17 November 2026** as its collection deadline. PBTE's circular gives **3 December 2026** for the single-fee online stage and **7 December 2026** for the corresponding printed documents and bank-deposited challan reaching the board. These are distinct actions and dates.

An institution may need time to check candidate records and prepare a batch before a board deadline. However, the supplied image does not document why 17 November was selected. Students should ask whether it is their institution's internal collection cutoff and what process applies if they miss it.

Do not ignore a notice received from your own examination office merely because a later board date exists. Equally, do not tell students at every affiliated college that PBTE has universally set 17 November as the single-fee deadline. That claim is not what the verified circular says.

## What are the board's single, double and triple stages?

The circular separates online feeding and fee submission from the later receipt of printed admission material. Keeping those steps separate is essential: a student paying an institution, the institution submitting information, and documents reaching PBTE are not the same event.

| Stage in the official circular | Online feeding and fee stage | Printed material reaching PBTE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single fee | 3 December 2026 | 7 December 2026 |
| Double fee | 10 December 2026 | 14 December 2026 |
| Triple fee | 17 December 2026 | 21 December 2026 |

The totals printed for these three stages are **Rs 3,700, Rs 5,840 and Rs 7,980**, respectively. Notice that the overall double-stage total is not simply twice 3,700. The circular doubles the theory and practical components while other listed components remain unchanged. Multiplying the whole single-fee total would produce an incorrect summary.

The document also contains a later entry with specific wording and conditions. Students should not treat that as an unrestricted extension. Ask the examination office to interpret any exceptional or late case against the full circular before relying on it.

## How can DIT students prepare while admission work is completed?

First, separate administrative readiness from study readiness. Keep the fee query, admission confirmation and timetable checks in one short checklist. Keep revision tasks in another. Completing a payment does not revise a chapter, while practising MCQs does not resolve an incorrect candidate record.

For learning support, open RayTech's [academic study resources](/classes/) and choose the relevant DIT material available for your subject. Use notes to understand a concept, then test recall with the related MCQs. Check the chapter title before assuming that a resource matches the syllabus your institution is assessing.

For example, when revising networking material, explain a term in your own words before checking an option. When revising office applications, connect a feature to its purpose rather than memorising only the wording of a question. These are study suggestions, not predictions about which questions PBTE will set.

If practical work is part of your assessed programme, ask the teacher which exercises and records need preparation. Do not assume that a set of online MCQs covers the entire examination. Written understanding, practical work and the institution's assessment instructions can involve different requirements.

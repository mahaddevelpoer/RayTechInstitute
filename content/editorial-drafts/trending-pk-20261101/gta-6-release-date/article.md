## How should you evaluate a preorder?

A preorder is a purchase decision, not a requirement to participate in launch discussion. Read what is being sold, what is included, how payment is handled and what happens if your plans change. Take a screenshot of the offer and its terms at the time of purchase, keeping personal account information out of anything you share publicly.

Avoid deciding solely from urgency language. A countdown or a message saying that only a few copies remain does not tell you whether the seller has reliable supply. Ask practical questions: is this a confirmed order or an expression of interest, is a delivery window stated, and is there a clear way to contact the seller after payment?

Do not confuse game ownership with unrelated merchandise, a collectible package, a gift card or a reservation deposit. If the offer is unclear, get the distinction resolved before payment. Product images can show several objects together while the written terms cover only one of them.

## How can you avoid misleading downloads and activation offers?

Treat promises of an early full-game download with caution, especially when they require an executable from an unrelated site, a browser extension, remote access or your account credentials. A video of gameplay does not establish that the person sharing a file can lawfully or safely supply the game.

Keep sign-in codes, recovery codes and passwords private. A seller who needs you to disclose a one-time code is asking for access beyond a normal product description. If an offer depends on temporarily disabling account protection, stop and review the transaction rather than following the instructions under pressure.

For parents and students, also separate age suitability from technical compatibility. Whether a console can run a title is a different question from whether its content is appropriate for a particular player. Review the product's rating and content information before treating it as an educational or child-friendly purchase.

## How do you build a useful series summary?

Create one row per completed match, with date, venue, result, batting totals and a source. Mark an unfinished game as unfinished rather than entering a predicted result. A series total should be calculated only from a defined set of completed records.

Then choose the question. You might compare opening partnerships, scoring in the final overs or bowling control during the middle phase. A table designed around one clear question is easier to interpret than a sheet containing every available number.

Record missing data explicitly. If one scorecard does not provide a phase split, do not substitute a guess from highlights. You can compare the remaining complete sample, provided the omission and sample size are stated.

Do not assume that a series ending provides a balanced experiment. Grounds, opponents selected and match situations can vary. Those differences help explain performance, but they limit claims about a permanent trend.

## Why are combined strike rates sometimes calculated incorrectly?

Combined batting strike rate uses total runs divided by total balls, multiplied by one hundred. It is not normally the simple average of the individual innings percentages. Longer innings contribute more balls to the combined sample.

For a fictional example, a batter makes ten from five balls and fifty from forty. The individual rates are 200 and 125. Their simple average is 162.5, but the combined contribution is sixty from forty-five, giving 133.33.

The combined figure answers how quickly runs were scored across the whole sample. The individual figures show variation between innings. Both are useful; they should not be substituted for each other.

This distinction is central to [batting and ranking analysis](/blogs/sahibzada-farhan-icc-t20i-rankings-explained/). A player's rating is another measure again and should not be reconstructed by adding the series runs to a rankings total.

## What changes when you compare bowling spells?

Convert overs to legal deliveries before combining rates. A display of 3.4 means twenty-two balls, not 3.4 decimal overs. Add the legal balls, then calculate the conceded runs per six deliveries.

Suppose two fictional spells are 3.4 overs for 25 and four overs for 30. Together they contain forty-six legal balls and concede fifty-five. The combined economy is approximately 7.17, calculated as fifty-five times six divided by forty-six.

Keep wickets and phase assignments alongside that rate. A player bowling at the end may face more aggressive batting than one operating during a rebuilding period. The rate describes scoring against the bowler; it does not fully describe the task.

If a dismissal is a run-out, do not add it to the bowler's wicket total. A correct team total can still produce an incorrect individual summary if the dismissal category is overlooked.

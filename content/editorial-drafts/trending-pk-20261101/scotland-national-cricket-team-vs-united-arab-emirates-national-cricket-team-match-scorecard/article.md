## Why does one report mention a different run margin?

The organiser's finals-day prose describes the same totals but uses 46 in its margin wording. Its structured result feed instead says Scotland won by 45 runs, which matches 135 minus 90. This guide uses the reconciled 45-run figure and identifies the inconsistency rather than copying both numbers without explanation.

The likely source of confusion is the difference between a winning target and a winning margin. In a straightforward chase of a completed 135, the target would be 136. A final 90 is 46 short of that target, while the defending side's winning margin is 135 minus 90, or 45.

Those are different questions. “How many more runs would have reached the target?” and “By how many runs did the first side win?” do not have to produce the same number. A one-run gap between them is expected in the simple example, because equalling the first total is not the same as exceeding it.

A numerical mismatch is a reason to inspect the labels, not an invitation to declare every source unreliable.

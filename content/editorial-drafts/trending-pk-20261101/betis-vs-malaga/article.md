## Why is attendance different from stadium capacity?

The league record includes an attendance figure for this match. Attendance describes the reported crowd at the event; capacity describes how many spectators a venue can accommodate under the relevant arrangements. A full-looking photograph, a historical capacity listing and a match attendance are not interchangeable evidence.

Suppose an invented venue has an available match capacity of 40,000 and a reported crowd of 30,000. Dividing attendance by capacity gives 75 percent, provided the two figures refer to compatible arrangements. Using an old maximum capacity for a differently configured event would produce a number that looks exact but answers the wrong question.

This guide does not calculate an occupancy percentage for La Cartuja because that would require a matched, verified capacity figure for this event. The decision to omit an unsupported calculation is part of accurate reporting. More decimal places cannot compensate for a denominator that has not been established.

For a visual project, use different labels and icons for crowd, seats and ticket sales rather than one generic “fans” box. Ticket allocations and people present are different quantities too.

## How many legal deliveries is 5.3 overs?

With six balls per over, 5.3 means five completed overs and three additional legal balls: thirty-three legal deliveries. It is not the decimal number 5.3 overs. ## Can unused balls be converted into hypothetical runs?

Not as a statement of what actually happened. You may explore a labelled scenario about what could have been scored, but the completed result contains only recorded runs. Multiplying unused balls by a convenient average does not create an alternative official total or prove the losing side would have caught the target.

For an original example, imagine a side ends with three balls unused and an earlier average of two runs per ball. Adding six hypothetical runs is one scenario, not a prediction guaranteed by that average. The next deliveries could have produced several outcomes, and the reason the innings ended may mean those balls were never available to play.

This is the difference between description and a counterfactual model. The description records the event; the model asks what might happen under changed assumptions. Label the assumptions before presenting a graph. Otherwise, an attractive projection can be mistaken for runs that a team was somehow owed.

A useful classroom question is whether the scenario changes the rules as well as the score. If the model lets an already-ended innings continue, it is no longer simply forecasting the same state. Making that change explicit helps learners recognise how a small-looking assumption can alter the entire question.

A simple recorded scoring rate describes runs relative to the relevant played allocation.

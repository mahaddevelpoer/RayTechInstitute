## How do you convert rupees in thousands into billions?

The statement's revenue entry is 132,418,817 under a rupees-in-thousands heading. Each displayed unit represents one thousand rupees. To express the same value in billions of rupees, divide the displayed number by one million, giving 132.418817 billion, or approximately 132.419 billion after rounding.

The conversion can feel counterintuitive because the heading has already removed three zeroes. You are not starting with a plain-rupee figure. Multiplying or dividing by a thousand without first reading that heading can create a result that is a thousand times too large or too small.

Practise with a fictional table entry of 2,500,000 in rupees thousands. Its plain-rupee value is 2.5 billion. Write the unit beside every stage rather than keeping only the digits. The written unit tells the next reader why the transformation is correct.

EPS is the important exception in this example. It is already expressed in rupees per share, so it should not be divided by one million along with the revenue column. A spreadsheet routine that applies one conversion to every numeric field can therefore produce a tidy but misleading table.

## What is the year-on-year revenue change?

Using the filing's unrounded values, revenue increased by approximately 37.66% relative to the comparable quarter. The calculation subtracts the old value from the new value, divides by the old value and multiplies by 100. The denominator is the earlier quarter, not the new one.

The profit comparison calculated in the same way is approximately 52.76%. These are calculations from the reported figures, not management forecasts. They describe the selected historical comparison and do not establish that the same growth will occur next quarter.

For a fictional exercise, revenue rises from 80 to 100. The increase is 20, and the growth rate is 25% because 20 is divided by 80. Dividing by 100 instead produces 20%, which answers a different question about the increase relative to the new value.

Keep rounded display values separate from calculation inputs. If you calculate with heavily rounded billions, the final percentage can differ slightly from one calculated with the full table entries. That does not automatically indicate an error, but the method should be clear.

## Does higher revenue prove higher production?

Revenue is a money measure. Production is an operational measure with its own physical units. A revenue change alone does not isolate how much came from output, realised prices, product mix or other relevant factors. To explain the cause, examine the operational information and management discussion rather than infer it from the headline total.

Consider a fictional business selling 1,000 units at 100 rupees each. Revenue is 100,000 rupees. If it later sells 1,100 units at 90 rupees each, volume rises by 10% while revenue falls to 99,000 rupees. Output and revenue have moved in different directions.

The example is arithmetic, not a claim about OGDCL's actual product prices or production. It shows why a single financial line cannot answer every operational question. A careful article distinguishes the measured result from an explanation that would require additional evidence.

The same discipline applies to an announcement about a field. A discovery, the start of production and a sustained production rate are different statements. Read what the actual notice confirms instead of treating every positive-sounding operational title as a complete long-term output forecast.

## Profit is not the same as cash collected

An income statement and a cash-flow statement serve different purposes. The SEC's beginner guide explains that an income statement describes earnings and expenses over a period, while a cash-flow statement reports cash movements. They are related records, but a profit figure is not simply the period's cash balance.

For a student, use a fictional sale on credit. A business may have a recorded sale while the customer has not yet paid the invoice. That example illustrates why you should not rename revenue "cash received" without checking the relevant accounting and collection information.

A complete review would look at the financial position, cash flows and notes alongside the profit-or-loss statement. This article does not invent a collection figure or diagnose a particular receivable from the selected revenue and profit rows.

Create separate notebook headings for revenue, profit, operating cash flow and cash balance. If you have not checked a field, leave it explicitly unchecked. Entering the profit number into all four boxes would not make the review more complete; it would erase the distinctions you need to understand.

## What does the declared PKR 5 dividend mean?

The October filing announces an interim cash dividend of PKR 5 per share and describes it as 50%. The PSX profile gives a face value of PKR 10. In that context, five rupees is half the face value; the percentage is not a promise of a 50% return on whatever market price a person pays.

Keep declaration, entitlement and actual payment distinct. The company's notice contains the relevant register and book-closure instructions. This guide does not determine a particular person's eligibility or replace those instructions with an inferred trading deadline.

A fictional example makes the percentage distinction clear. If a five-rupee distribution is compared with a hypothetical 200-rupee purchase price, the simple ratio is 2.5%. That calculation is not the same denominator as five divided by a ten-rupee face value. Neither calculation alone measures a complete investment return.

Price changes, timing and applicable costs can affect a real outcome. The useful lesson here is narrower: always identify the denominator behind a financial percentage. Do not copy the largest percentage on a notice into a personal-return claim.

## Why not multiply the quarter by four and call it next year's profit?

Multiplying a historical quarter by four produces a simple annualised arithmetic figure. It does not establish what will actually happen across four future quarters. The operation assumes a repetition that has not been proved by the multiplication itself.

For a fictional business, one quarter may contain an unusual sale or expense. A model can explore an assumption about repetition, but its output should remain labelled as a scenario. Presenting the result as an announced annual forecast would change the status of the information.

This distinction is useful in school spreadsheets. Put the assumption in a separate cell and label the output "scenario," not "reported result." That design lets another reader see which numbers came from a source and which came from a model you created.

It is equally important when comparing an annual summary with a new quarterly announcement. Match the periods before calculating a growth rate. A longer period normally covers more activity, so a bigger number alone does not establish a stronger performance.

## Make a source-first financial worksheet

Use columns for entity, reporting period, statement type, audit status, item, original value, original unit, converted value and source. Keep the original number even after conversion so a reviewer can trace your arithmetic back to the filing.

Add a formula description rather than relying on an unexplained spreadsheet result. For growth, identify the earlier-period denominator. For margin, identify which profit measure is being divided by which revenue measure. Do not mix gross profit with a profit-after-tax label.

From the selected quarter's profit and revenue, the simple ratio is approximately 44.19%. Label it as profit for the quarter divided by revenue, rather than an operating margin. An operating-profit calculation would require the appropriate operating-profit measure and its definition.

```python
def growth_percent(current, previous):
    if previous <= 0:
        raise ValueError('This simple growth example needs a positive base')
    return (current - previous) / previous * 100

print(growth_percent(100, 80))  # Fictional example: 25.0
```

The check prevents an unsuitable denominator from being hidden inside a plausible-looking result. It does not make the program a complete financial-analysis service. The definitions, source and period still need to be correct before the arithmetic becomes meaningful.

### Are OGDC and OGDCL references to different companies here?

In this guide, OGDC is the PSX symbol and a commonly used company reference, while OGDCL abbreviates Oil and Gas Development Company Limited. Confirm the entity through the official profile rather than assuming every similar abbreviation belongs to the same business.

### Is EPS the dividend amount?

No. Earnings per share and a declared dividend are different measures. A profit calculation does not automatically become a distribution to shareholders. Read the declaration separately and keep its unit and conditions intact.

### Does this page give a live share price?

No. A market quote needs its own session and timestamp. The financial figures discussed here belong to a specified reporting period; they should not be confused with a live trading screen or a recommendation about a transaction.

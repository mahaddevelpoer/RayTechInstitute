## A profile position and a match assignment answer different questions

A profile asks what kind of player the club generally presents. A match assignment asks where the player is used within a particular lineup. The two answers can differ without either being an error.

Consider an original school-team example. A student usually plays centrally but is selected on the right for one match. The season profile might still describe a midfielder, while that day's team sheet records a wider or deeper assignment.

The example does not reproduce Barcelona's private tactical plan. It simply explains why a positional label should be read at the right level of detail.

This also prevents a common reporting shortcut: describing someone as having “changed position permanently” after seeing one lineup. A permanent change needs stronger evidence than one match, especially for a player whose development includes several roles.

## What does versatility actually mean?

Versatility describes the ability to contribute in more than one role or situation. It is not the same as claiming that every role demands exactly the same skills.

A central midfielder and a right-back may both need sound passing and awareness, but they can receive the ball with different space, pressure and responsibilities around them. The useful viewing question is how the assignment changes the decisions available.

For an original observation exercise, choose a short passage and note three things: where the player receives, which options are visible and what happens after the next action. Describe those facts before reaching a broad conclusion about suitability.

This guide does not claim a firsthand technical scouting assessment of Fariñas' entire performance. It uses the reported assignment to introduce a way of watching more carefully. A full evaluation would need a clearer method and more evidence than a debut headline.

## Why a successful team result is not an individual rating

A 6–1 win records a team outcome. It does not automatically provide a score out of ten for every player, nor does it reveal how frequently a particular defender was tested.

When discussing an individual, identify the action or sequence that supports the claim. A completed match establishes playing time in that fixture. It does not by itself establish flawless passing, perfect positioning or the absence of mistakes.

The same principle works after a defeat. A losing result does not prove that every player's contribution was poor. Team outcomes are important context, but individual analysis needs evidence matched to the question.

For a classroom discussion, separate two sentences: “The team won” and “I think this action helped because…” The second sentence requires its own explanation rather than borrowing certainty from the first.

## Registration, squad membership and debut are separate milestones

The official profile records Fariñas joining the senior squad at the start of 2026/27. The October report records the subsequent debut. Those dates serve different purposes in a career timeline.

A player can belong to a squad before making an appearance. A place on a bench is also not the same as entering the field. A start and a substitute appearance are both appearances, but they describe different forms of participation.

Use separate fields in a notebook: squad status, match selection, start or substitute, and minutes where the source provides them. Do not turn a registration announcement into a claim that a competitive match has already been played.

This is a useful general information habit. Admission to a programme, attendance at a session and completion of a course are related milestones, but none should automatically be substituted for another.

Similarly, adding five goals and six assists produces 11 recorded goal contributions under those categories. It does not mean the player personally scored 11 goals or that each contribution measures an identical action.

Do not use these figures to invent a complete senior record. The correct scope is more valuable than a larger-looking number created by mixing youth, reserves and first-team competitions.

## Why per-appearance and per-90 rates differ

Here is a fictional example, separate from Fariñas' record. Player A scores once in two appearances, playing a combined 90 minutes. Player B scores once in two appearances, playing a combined 180 minutes.

Both have 0.5 goals per appearance. Their goals per 90 minutes differ: Player A has one, while Player B has 0.5. The difference comes from playing time, not from a disagreement about how many goals were scored.

The example explains why a statistic needs its definition. A table with a heading saying only “rate” does not give enough information for a reliable comparison.

For a young player with limited appearances, also consider how small the sample is. One additional action can change a rate sharply. That is a reason to communicate uncertainty, not a reason to dismiss the milestone or exaggerate its predictive power.

## How to follow a young player without turning development into a forecast

Start with dated evidence: a match report, a squad notice or an official profile. Then describe what the evidence establishes now, rather than extending it into a guaranteed future role.

Questions such as “Will he always start?” or “Will he become the next famous midfielder?” concern outcomes that a debut report cannot settle. Comparing names may be entertaining, but it should not replace analysis of the actual player.

A more useful development notebook tracks opportunities, roles and clearly sourced contributions over time. It leaves room for a changing assignment and for the normal uncertainty of a sporting career.

Avoid rumours about private conversations, contracts or selection promises unless a reliable source directly supports them. A photograph in training cannot reveal every decision a coach will make before the next fixture.

## A practical viewing worksheet

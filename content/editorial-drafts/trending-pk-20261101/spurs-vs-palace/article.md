## An upgraded card is not a second yellow

A second-yellow dismissal involves a player receiving another caution after an earlier caution. An upgrade after a review concerns the sanction for the incident being reviewed. The words describe different paths to a sending-off.

For this match, the official account describes a straight-red upgrade. Do not turn the initially displayed yellow into a separate earlier offence and write that Mateta received two yellows.

In a fictional timeline, an incident might first be marked “yellow, under review” and later “straight red, decision confirmed.” A well-organised record updates the status of that incident rather than creating a second unrelated foul at the same time.

This guide reports the published decision. It does not claim to know the officials' private discussion or provide a new judgment from a short clip. A clear description can explain the distinction without pretending to overrule the match officials.

## Why Palace could not simply replace the dismissed player

Law 3 states that a player sent off after kickoff cannot be replaced. That is why an on-field dismissal during play changes the number of players available to the team on the pitch.

An ordinary substitution is different. In a substitution, one player leaves and an eligible substitute enters under the applicable procedure. It does not restore a player lost to a post-kickoff sending-off.

This also explains why the timing and identity of a dismissal matter. A named substitute or a team official being dismissed is not identical to an active player being sent off during the match.

The useful beginner habit is to ask three questions: who was dismissed, when did it happen and what rule applies to replacement? Do not assume every red-card icon changes the on-field player count in exactly the same way.

## A clean sheet does not tell you how every attack ended

Tottenham conceded no goals in this fixture. That establishes a clean sheet at team level. It does not establish that the goalkeeper faced no work, that no opponent reached a dangerous area or that every defensive action was perfect.

To make one of those narrower claims, look for the corresponding evidence. A save needs a recorded or observed effort. A blocked shot needs a different event. An unsuccessful final pass may never become a shot at all.

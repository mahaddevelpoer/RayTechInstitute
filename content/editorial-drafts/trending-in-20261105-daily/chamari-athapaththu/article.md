## Why does 44.3 overs not mean 44.3 decimal overs?

In conventional cricket score notation, 44.3 means forty-four completed overs and three further legal balls, not forty-four and three-tenths of an over. With six legal balls per over, that corresponds to 267 legal deliveries. The dot separates overs and balls rather than introducing an ordinary decimal fraction.

This distinction matters when calculating a rate. Entering 44.3 directly into a decimal calculator as a number of overs produces a different denominator from forty-four and a half overs. For a worked exercise, first convert the notation into legal balls, then apply the intended rate formula consistently.

The legal-ball count also differs from simply counting every delivery event, because extras can affect how deliveries are counted. An introductory calculation should state its scope. A careful learner checks the scorecard notation before assuming that a number written with a dot behaves like a decimal measurement in another subject.

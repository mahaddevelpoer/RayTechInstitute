## What is effective field-goal percentage?

Effective field-goal percentage adjusts the made-field-goal calculation to account for the extra point from a three-pointer. Using the conventional formula, add half the made three-pointers to made field goals, divide by field-goal attempts and multiply by 100. It is a defined scoring measure, not a new category of basket.

For the recorded line, the calculation is eleven plus half of six, giving fourteen. Fourteen divided by 23 is approximately 60.87 percent. The result differs from ordinary field-goal percentage because six of the makes carried three points rather than two. It does not include the free-throw contribution.

Label the measure when using it. Printing 60.87 percent without effective field-goal wording could make readers think it is the ordinary shooting percentage. Two correct calculations can look contradictory if the headings disappear. A clear statistical explanation preserves both the formula and the name.

## How to compare two player lines responsibly

Choose the same competition and period, then keep minutes, attempts and roles visible where available. A player taking more attempts can score more points while shooting a lower percentage. That is not a contradiction; it means volume and accuracy are different dimensions of the record.

Do not average percentages blindly across games. A fictional one-for-two game and a nine-for-ten game combine to ten makes from twelve attempts, about 83.33 percent. The simple average of their 50 and 90 percent figures would be 70, which does not match the combined attempts. The denominator matters.

When building a spreadsheet, store makes and attempts separately and calculate the rate from totals. That makes later corrections easier and avoids a common statistical error. The same method applies to exam results, survey response rates and other situations where groups have different sizes.
